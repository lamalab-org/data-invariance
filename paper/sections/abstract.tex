\begin{abstract}
Retraining a chemistry classifier on a fresh draw of the same training
population flips predictions for $8\text{--}17\%$ of test molecules
with no change in aggregate accuracy.  This is the population-level
analogue of \emph{prediction churn}~\citep{TODO_milani2016}: where the
deployment-loop literature measures churn between a new model and a
fixed incumbent, we measure it between two equally weighted
retrainings on independent bootstraps of the same training pool, in
the small-$N$ from-scratch regime where no incumbent model exists.
We call this quantity \emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}.  On
eight chemistry benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox, materials
science) it dominates the parameter-variance axis that deep ensembles
capture by an order of magnitude.  Bootstrap bagging is the right
intervention on the data axis; on the small-$N$ chemistry datasets we
study it cuts churn $47\text{--}49\%$ at no accuracy regression.
Adding a sym-KL consistency loss between the two bagging networks
(\emph{twin-indep}) cuts churn $69\text{--}72\%$ at matched compute
and distributional disagreement by an additional order of magnitude;
twin-indep is a $\sim$$40\%$-bootstrap-overlap operating point on the
codistillation family, and is the only point on that family that does
not catastrophically fail across 10 datasets we test.  The recipe is
small-$N$ from-scratch; on pretrained-backbone benchmarks like
Waterbirds the consistency loss over-regularises features the
backbone already encodes.
\end{abstract}
