\begin{abstract}
Scientific machine learning reports accuracy.  It does not report
whether the same prediction would survive a different draw of
training data.  Across $\nDatasetsHeadline$ chemistry benchmarks,
two classifiers trained on independent bootstraps of the same
training set agree on aggregate accuracy to within
$\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$ percentage points but disagree on
the class label of $\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test
molecules.  We call this gap \emph{cross-sample prediction churn}.
Prior churn work measures disagreement between a deployed model and
its challenger at web scale; we measure disagreement between models
retrained from scratch on different bootstraps of the same small
training set --- the regime that drug discovery, materials, and
biostatistics actually inhabit.  The standard parameter-side
techniques (deep ensembles, MC dropout, stochastic weight averaging)
do not reduce this gap; they sample over weights at fixed data, the
orthogonal axis.  Two data-side methods do.  Bagging --- a classical
baseline that has not previously been evaluated for churn reduction
in this regime --- cuts the rate $\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$
on every dataset at no accuracy cost ($K\times$-ERM compute).  We propose \emph{twin-bootstrap} --- two
networks trained on independent bootstraps with a sym-KL consistency
loss between their predictions --- which cuts a further median
$\twinFurtherMedianReductionVsBagTwo\%$ beyond bagging-$K{=}2$ at
matched $2\times$-ERM compute.  Churn deserves a column alongside
accuracy in scientific-ML benchmark reports.
\end{abstract}
