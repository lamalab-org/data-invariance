\begin{abstract}
Two classifiers trained on independent samples of the same chemistry
training population assign different classes to $8\text{--}22\%$ of
test molecules; aggregate accuracy is unchanged.  We call this
\emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}: the population-level
analogue of \emph{prediction churn}, measured
between two equally weighted bootstraps in the small-$N$ from-scratch
regime where scientific ML retrains and where no incumbent exists.
Deep ensembles and MC dropout vary parameters at fixed data; they
leave argmax churn unchanged ($-13\%$ to $+7\%$ relative on nine
chemistry benchmarks).  Methods that vary the data work.  $K$-bootstrap
bagging cuts argmax churn $40\text{--}54\%$ on every dataset with no
accuracy regression.  \emph{Twin-indep} --- codistillation at $\sim$$40\%$ inter-network
bootstrap overlap --- cuts a further median $60\%$ (range
$16\text{--}92\%$) at matched $2\times$-ERM compute against
bagging-$K{=}2$, and reduces distributional disagreement by another
factor of ${\sim}8$; across three overlap points ($0\%$, $\sim$$40\%$,
$\sim$$63\%$), only $\sim$$40\%$ never increases churn relative to
ERM.  The selection rule that picks $\lambda$ on the development
dataset transfers across all three architectures and modalities we
tested (from-scratch GIN, ChemBERTa-77M-MTR, ImageNet-pretrained
ResNet-50); the numerical $\lambda$ does not.  Bagging and
codistillation already exist; what this paper adds is a measurement
protocol that makes their effect on cross-sample fragility visible,
and a regime where the gap they close is large enough to demand its
own column in the benchmark report.
\end{abstract}
