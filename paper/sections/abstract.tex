\begin{abstract}
Scientific machine learning reports accuracy.  It does not report
whether the same prediction would survive a different draw of
training data.  Across $\nDatasetsHeadline$ chemistry benchmarks,
two classifiers trained on independent bootstraps of the same
training set agree on aggregate accuracy to within
$\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$ percentage points but disagree on
the class label of $\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test
molecules.  We call this gap \emph{cross-sample prediction churn} ---
the data-sampling analogue of the prediction churn measured at web
scale by deployment-loop work, in the small-$N$ from-scratch regime
where deployment-loop tools do not apply.  The standard
parameter-side techniques (deep ensembles, MC dropout, stochastic
weight averaging) do not reduce it; they vary weights at fixed data,
the wrong axis.  Two data-side methods do.  Bagging cuts the rate
$\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ at no accuracy cost.
\emph{Twin-bootstrap} --- two networks trained on independent
bootstraps with a sym-KL consistency loss --- cuts a further median
$\twinFurtherMedianReductionVsBagTwo\%$ beyond bagging-$K{=}2$ at
matched $2\times$-ERM compute.  The contribution is in two parts:
a measurement protocol for the small-$N$ regime, and two training
procedures that reduce what it measures.  Churn deserves a column
alongside accuracy in scientific-ML benchmark reports.
\end{abstract}
