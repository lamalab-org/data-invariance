\begin{abstract}
Retraining a chemistry classifier on a fresh draw of the same training
population flips predictions for $8\text{--}17\%$ of test molecules
with no change in aggregate accuracy.  We name this \emph{cross-sample
prediction fragility} --- the fraction of test predictions that change
class between two models trained on independent bootstraps --- and
show it dominates the parameter-variance axis that deep ensembles
capture by an order of magnitude on eight standard chemistry
benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox, materials science).  Two
methods reduce it under a strict no-test-tuning protocol.
$K$-bootstrap bagging cuts churn $47\text{--}49\%$ at zero accuracy
cost.  \emph{Twin-indep}, a sym-KL consistency penalty between two
networks trained on independent bootstraps, cuts churn
$69\text{--}72\%$ at matched compute and distributional disagreement
by roughly an additional order of magnitude.  Twin-indep applies in
the small-$N$ from-scratch regime that scientific ML inhabits; on
pretrained-backbone benchmarks like Waterbirds the consistency loss
over-regularises features the backbone already encodes.
\end{abstract}
