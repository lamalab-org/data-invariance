\begin{abstract}
Two classifiers trained on independent samples of the same chemistry
training population assign different classes to $8\text{--}22\%$ of
test molecules; aggregate accuracy is unchanged.  We call this
\emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}: the population-level
analogue of \emph{prediction churn}, measured between two bootstraps
of the same training pool.  Deep ensembles and MC dropout vary
parameters at fixed data; the class-flip rate barely moves ($-13\%$
to $+7\%$ relative across nine chemistry benchmarks).  Methods that
vary the data work.  Bagging cuts the rate $40\text{--}54\%$ on every
dataset with no accuracy regression.  \emph{Twin-bootstrap} --- two
networks trained on independent bootstraps with a soft consistency
loss between their predictions --- cuts a further median $60\%$
(range $16\text{--}92\%$) at twice ERM compute, and shrinks the
softmax-level disagreement by another factor of ${\sim}8$.
Twin-bootstrap is the codistillation family at $\sim$$40\%$
inter-network bootstrap overlap; among the three overlap points we
tested ($0\%$, $\sim$$40\%$, $\sim$$63\%$), the middle point is the
only one that never makes the class-flip rate worse.  The selection
rule for the consistency-loss weight transfers across all three
architectures we tested (a from-scratch GIN, ChemBERTa,
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50); the value the rule picks does not.
The methods exist already; what this paper adds is a measurement
protocol that makes their effect on cross-sample fragility visible,
and a regime where the gap they close is large enough to demand its
own column in the benchmark report.
\end{abstract}
