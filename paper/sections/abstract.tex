\begin{abstract}
Two classifiers trained on independent bootstraps of the same
chemistry training set assign different classes to
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test molecules, while their
aggregate accuracy differs by only $\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$
percentage points.  We call this \emph{cross-sample prediction
churn}.  Across \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks the
standard parameter-side techniques --- deep ensembles, Monte Carlo
dropout, and stochastic weight averaging (SWA) --- shift the
class-flip rate by $\paramSideLow\%$ to $\paramSideHigh\%$ relative
to ERM, with no consistent sign.  Methods that vary the training-data sample do
better.  Bagging cuts the rate
$\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ on every dataset at no
accuracy cost.  \emph{Twin-bootstrap} --- two networks trained on
independent bootstraps with a soft consistency loss --- cuts a
further median $\twinFurtherMedianReductionVsBagTwo\%$ beyond
bagging-$K{=}2$ at matched $2\times$-ERM compute.  Cross-sample churn belongs alongside accuracy in
small-$N$ scientific-ML benchmark reports.
\end{abstract}
