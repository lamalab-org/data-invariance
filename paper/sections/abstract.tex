\begin{abstract}
Two classifiers trained on independent bootstraps of the same
chemistry training set assign different classes to
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test molecules, while their
aggregate accuracy differs by only $\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$
percentage points.  We call this \emph{cross-sample prediction
fragility}.  The standard parameter-side uncertainty methods ---
deep ensembles and Monte Carlo dropout --- fail to reduce it: across
\nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks they move the class-flip
rate by $\paramSideLow\%$ to $\paramSideHigh\%$ relative to ERM,
with no consistent direction.  Methods that vary the training-data
sample work.  Bagging cuts the rate
$\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ on every dataset at no accuracy
cost; \emph{twin-bootstrap} --- two networks trained on independent
bootstraps with a soft consistency loss --- cuts a further median
$\twinMedianReduction\%$ at matched $2\times$-ERM compute.  The gap
is large enough that cross-sample fragility belongs alongside
accuracy in small-$N$ scientific-ML benchmark reports.
\end{abstract}
