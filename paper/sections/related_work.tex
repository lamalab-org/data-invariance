\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

We organise prior work by what it does \emph{not} address.

\paragraph{Algorithmic stability.}
\citet{TODO_bousquet2002} bound the generalisation gap by uniform
stability $\beta = O(1/N)$ but treat $\beta$ as an analytical quantity.
We measure it as a per-example, deployment-relevant signal and use it
as a training objective.

\paragraph{Parameter-distribution uncertainty.}
Deep ensembles~\citep{TODO_lakshminarayanan2017} and MC
dropout~\citep{TODO_gal2016} sample over model parameters at a
\emph{fixed} dataset.  Our measurements (Section~\ref{sec:measurement})
show this is the wrong axis: deep ensembles reduce per-example churn by
${\sim}9\%$ on average, while the data axis we measure has
$10\text{--}100\times$ more variance.

\paragraph{Bagging.}
\citet{TODO_breiman1996} introduced bootstrap aggregation as a
variance-reduction estimator.  In modern deep-learning practice it has
been displaced by deep ensembling (same compute, no resampling).  We
revisit bagging and show it is the strongest no-cost baseline for
cross-sample stability; the data-resampling axis is precisely the one
deep ensembles miss.

\paragraph{Consistency regularisation.}
Mean Teacher and $\Pi$-model~\citep{TODO_tarvainen2017} penalise
disagreement under \emph{input augmentation}.  We penalise disagreement
under \emph{training-data resampling}, which is a different axis.
The two are complementary.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction.}
\citet{TODO_vovk2005} provide marginal coverage guarantees but
make no statement about per-example prediction stability under
retraining.

\paragraph{Worst-group robustness.}
Group DRO~\citep{TODO_sagawa2020},
IRM~\citep{TODO_arjovsky2019}, and
V-REx~\citep{TODO_krueger2021} address worst-group accuracy under
distribution shift.  Cross-sample fragility is orthogonal: a perfectly
group-invariant model can still flip individual predictions on
retraining of the same population.

\paragraph{Prediction churn.}
Industry studies~\citep{TODO_milani2016, TODO_anil2023} document
prediction churn as a deployment problem and propose ad-hoc remedies
(warm-starting, distillation against the previous model).  We provide
a principled definition tied to algorithmic stability, a measurement
protocol with paired confidence intervals, and a training-time
intervention that does not require access to the previous model.
