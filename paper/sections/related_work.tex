\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Prediction churn.}
\citet{TODO_milani2016} introduced \emph{prediction churn} as the
disagreement between two classifiers trained on the same task with
different runs or data updates and proposed an MCMC operator that
regularises a new model toward a deployed one.
\citet{TODO_bhojanapalli2021} dissect the sources of churn on CIFAR
and ImageNet and isolate initialisation and data-order randomness;
\citet{TODO_jiang2022} prove an equivalence between distillation
from a fixed reference model and an explicit churn constraint, and
report that distillation is the strongest churn-reduction mechanism
in deployment settings.  This paper inherits the per-example framing
and the term ``churn''.  The gap we fill: prior work measures churn
in the deployment loop (a new model regressed against an incumbent
on web-scale data); cross-sample fragility measures churn at the
population level (two equally weighted retrainings on independent
samples of the same population), in the small-$N$ from-scratch
regime where a frozen reference model does not exist.

\paragraph{Predictive multiplicity and underspecification.}
\citet{TODO_marx2020} define \emph{predictive multiplicity} as the
existence of equally accurate models that disagree on individual
predictions; \citet{TODO_damour2022} argue under-specified pipelines
routinely produce them; \citet{TODO_black2022} use ensemble
disagreement to abstain on contested predictions at deployment.
These works study disagreement across the loss-equivalent model
class at a fixed dataset.  Cross-sample fragility studies the
complementary slice --- disagreement across independent draws of the
dataset --- and is the slice the practitioner sees between
retrainings on a refreshed sample.  Both can be read under the
aleatoric/epistemic taxonomy of~\citet{TODO_huellermeier2021}.

\paragraph{Parameter-side uncertainty and bagging.}
Deep ensembles~\citep{TODO_lakshminarayanan2017} and MC
dropout~\citep{TODO_gal2016} sample over model parameters at a fixed
dataset.  Bagging~\citep{TODO_breiman1996}, in the lineage of
stability selection~\citep{TODO_meinshausen2010}, samples over the
data; \citet{TODO_nixon2020} report that bagged neural-network
ensembles trade some accuracy for the resampling.  The data-resample
axis is the right one for cross-sample fragility, and our small-$N$
experiments find no accuracy regression from bagging in this regime.
Bootstrapped DQN~\citep{TODO_osband2016} uses a closely related
multi-head bootstrap construction for exploration in reinforcement
learning.

\paragraph{Two-network training with a disagreement loss.}
\citet{TODO_anil2018_codistillation} train two networks on disjoint
data shards with a mutual KL agreement loss to accelerate
large-scale training, reporting churn reduction as a side benefit.
\citet{TODO_han2018_coteaching} use a two-network construction with
disagreement-driven sample selection for label-noise robustness;
mean teacher and $\Pi$-model~\citep{TODO_tarvainen2017} penalise
disagreement under input augmentation rather than training-data
resampling.  Twin-indep is mechanically closest to codistillation
(two networks, KL agreement) but operates at $\sim$$40\%$
inter-network bootstrap overlap rather than at the disjoint-shard
$0\%$ end of the same axis; on a 10-dataset comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}) codistillation degrades
id-accuracy beyond a $5$pp threshold on Pgp and MOF-thermal,
twin-shared introduces a positive churn delta on DILI, and
twin-indep at ${\sim}40\%$ is the only column that never increases
churn relative to ERM.

\paragraph{Clinical-biostatistics precedent.}
\citet{TODO_riley2023} develop a per-individual
\emph{stability-interval} program for clinical prediction models
and show that bootstrap-resampled point estimates can span the unit
interval for the same patient.  The framing is the closest published
precedent for the deployment-driven argument we make in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}, in a different domain and without a
deep-learning treatment.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction, group robustness, and data attribution.}
Conformal prediction~\citep{TODO_vovk2005} provides marginal
coverage guarantees over the test point given a fixed training set,
not stability across resamples of the training set.  A conformal
wrapper preserves the underlying point prediction --- argmax churn
of an ERM model wrapped with split conformal is identical to argmax
churn of the bare ERM model --- so we report ERM as the conformal
baseline rather than running a separate column.  Group-robust
methods~\citep{TODO_sagawa2020, TODO_arjovsky2019, TODO_krueger2021}
target accuracy on rare subpopulations.  Influence
functions~\citep{TODO_koh2017_influence, TODO_pruthi2020_tracin} and
datamodels~\citep{TODO_ilyas2022_datamodels} attribute a prediction
to the training examples that most influenced it; cross-sample
fragility is the corresponding output-side signal and requires
only $K$ retrainings rather than the ${\sim}10^5$ models a datamodel
surrogate is fit on.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology.}
\citet{TODO_bouthillier2021} argue for accounting for multiple
variance sources in ML benchmarks; this paper pairs every reported
delta across $\binom{10}{2}=45$ seed pairs and uses
$10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the seed-pair distribution to compute
$95\%$ confidence intervals.
