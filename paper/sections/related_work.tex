\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

We position cross-sample prediction fragility against four bodies of
prior work: deployment-driven prediction-churn studies, predictive
multiplicity in the Rashomon sense, parameter-side uncertainty
quantification, and methods for two-network training with a
disagreement loss.

\paragraph{Prediction churn.}
\citet{TODO_milani2016} introduced the term \emph{prediction churn} for
the disagreement between two classifiers trained on the same task with
different runs or data updates, and proposed an MCMC stabilisation
operator that regularises a new model toward a deployed one.
\citet{TODO_bhojanapalli2021} dissect the sources of churn empirically
on CIFAR and ImageNet, isolating initialisation and data-order
randomness; the data-resample axis is mentioned but not the focus.
\citet{TODO_jiang2022} prove an equivalence between distillation from
a fixed reference model and an explicit churn constraint, and report
that distillation is the strongest churn-reduction mechanism in the
deployment setting.  We adopt the term and the per-example framing,
and extend the literature in three directions: (i)~from deployment
churn against an incumbent model to the population-level fragility
between two equally weighted retrainings on independent samples,
(ii)~from web-scale settings to small-$N$ from-scratch scientific
machine learning, and (iii)~from aggregate churn rates to per-example
fragility usable as a deployment-time triage signal.

\paragraph{Predictive multiplicity and underspecification.}
\citet{TODO_marx2020} define \emph{predictive multiplicity} as the
existence of equally accurate models that disagree on individual
predictions, and \citet{TODO_damour2022} argue that under-specified
ML pipelines routinely produce such disagreeing predictors.
\citet{TODO_black2022} operationalise this at deployment time, using
ensemble disagreement to abstain on contested predictions.  These
works study disagreement across the loss-equivalent model class at a
fixed dataset; cross-sample fragility studies disagreement across
independent draws of the dataset.  The two are complementary slices of
the same epistemic uncertainty
question~\citep{TODO_huellermeier2021}.

\paragraph{Parameter-side uncertainty and bagging.}
Deep ensembles~\citep{TODO_lakshminarayanan2017} and MC
dropout~\citep{TODO_gal2016} sample over model parameters at a
\emph{fixed} dataset; we measure (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}) that
the data-resample axis they miss accounts for an order of magnitude
more per-example variability than their parameter axis.  Bagging, the
classical bootstrap variance-reduction estimator
of~\citet{TODO_breiman1996} and the conceptual lineage of stability
selection~\citep{TODO_meinshausen2010}, has been argued to be a
\emph{worse} accuracy estimator for neural networks than full-data
ensembles because each member sees only ${\sim}63\%$ unique
indices~\citep{TODO_nixon2020}.  For the deliverable that matters here --- per-example stability
across retrainings --- bagging is the right intervention, and on the
small-$N$ chemistry benchmarks of this paper we additionally observe
a small accuracy gain over ERM rather than the regression
\citet{TODO_nixon2020} report at scale.
Bootstrapped DQN~\citep{TODO_osband2016} uses a closely related
multi-head bootstrap construction for exploration in reinforcement
learning.

\paragraph{Two-network training with a disagreement loss.}
\citet{TODO_anil2018_codistillation} train two (or more) networks on
typically \emph{disjoint} data shards with a mutual KL agreement loss
to accelerate large-scale training, and report churn reduction as a
side benefit.  Co-teaching~\citep{TODO_han2018_coteaching} uses a
two-network construction with disagreement-driven sample selection
for label-noise robustness.  Mean Teacher and
$\Pi$-model~\citep{TODO_tarvainen2017} penalise disagreement under
\emph{input augmentation}, not training-data resampling.  Twin-indep
shares mechanics with codistillation --- two networks, KL agreement
loss --- but differs in protocol: bootstraps with replacement
($\sim$$40\%$ overlap between the pair) rather than disjoint shards
($0\%$ overlap), small-$N$ from-scratch rather than web-scale, and
per-example fragility as the deliverable rather than a side metric.
The protocol difference is a single hyperparameter, the
inter-network bootstrap-overlap fraction, on which the two methods
sit at opposite ends.  Adding the twin-indep-with-shared-bootstrap
ablation ($100\%$ overlap; only initialisation differs between the
two networks) gives us three operating points on this spectrum and
their churn-reduction CIs (Figure~\ref{fig:overlap}).
Codistillation matches or beats twin-indep on $5$ of $6$ datasets
that pass the consistency-loss filter; on the fifth (TADF)
codistillation flips sign to $+3.0$pp $[+1.3, +5.0]$ versus ERM
--- $i.e.$\ \emph{worse} than no method --- where the disjoint-shard
protocol leaves each network with only $N/2 {\approx} 500$ unique
training examples.  Twin-indep matches or beats ERM on every
dataset.  The bootstrap-overlap fraction is therefore a
robustness-vs-best-case trade-off; twin-indep's overlapping
bootstraps sit at the safe operating point that never catastrophically
fails.  Whether dataset-adaptive overlap can recover codistillation's
gains without TADF's failure is open.

\paragraph{Clinical-biostatistics precedent.}
\citet{TODO_riley2023} develop a stability-interval program for
clinical prediction models, arguing that bootstrap-based per-individual
stability plots reveal predictions whose point estimates span $0.1$ to
$0.8$ across resamples and are consequently unreliable for
patient-level decisions.  The framing is the closest published
precedent for our deployment-driven argument, in a different domain
and without a deep-learning treatment.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction, group robustness, and data attribution.}
Conformal prediction~\citep{TODO_vovk2005} provides marginal coverage
under a fixed training set rather than stability across resamples of
it.  Worst-group methods~\citep{TODO_sagawa2020,
TODO_arjovsky2019, TODO_krueger2021} target accuracy on rare
subpopulations, a different axis from per-example retraining churn.
Influence functions~\citep{TODO_koh2017_influence,
TODO_pruthi2020_tracin} and datamodels~\citep{TODO_ilyas2022_datamodels}
identify the training examples whose inclusion most affects a given
prediction; per-example fragility is the corresponding output-side
diagnostic and requires only one additional model rather than the
${\sim}10^5$ models a datamodel surrogate is fit on.

\paragraph{Evaluation methodology.}
We follow the multi-source variance accounting argued for
by~\citet{TODO_bouthillier2021}: every reported delta is paired across
$\binom{10}{2}=45$ seed pairs, and confidence intervals are computed
by $10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the seed-pair distribution.
