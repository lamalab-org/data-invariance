\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What practitioners should do.}
For deployed scientific machine learning at $N \lesssim 5000$ from
scratch, run $K$-bootstrap bagging with $K{=}5$ as the default.  This
removes about half of the per-example prediction churn that retraining
would otherwise produce, at a $5\times$ training-time cost and zero
accuracy cost.  When more stability is needed and the fivefold
training cost is acceptable, twin-indep at the BACE-frozen
$\lambda{=}300$ removes the next layer of churn at a $2\times$ cost.

\paragraph{Position relative to prior work.}
Cross-sample fragility is the missing axis in the standard uncertainty
toolkit.  Deep ensembles, MC dropout, conformal prediction, and the
group-robustness literature each address a different axis of prediction
uncertainty.  Cross-sample fragility is the one that corresponds to
the deployment quantity practitioners actually care about: whether a
prediction will change at the next retraining.

\paragraph{Future work.}
A natural next step is regression: cross-sample fragility extends with
an MAE or covered-interval metric in place of argmax disagreement,
and the consistency loss becomes an MSE or Huber penalty.  Connecting
twin-indep to influence-function-based data attribution is also
natural: per-example fragility is a soft measure of how influential
the most-influential training examples are.  Finally, replacing the
fixed $\lambda$ with a principled scaling
$\lambda_N \propto 1/N$ derived from algorithmic stability would
remove even the single hyperparameter we currently set on the
development dataset.
