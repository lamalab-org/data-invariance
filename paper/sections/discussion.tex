\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What practitioners should do.}
For deployed scientific machine learning at $N \lesssim 5000$ from
scratch, run $K$-bootstrap bagging with $K{=}5$ as the default.  This
removes about half of the per-example prediction churn that retraining
would otherwise produce, at a $5\times$ training-time cost and a
small accuracy improvement on average ($+1.6$pp pooled across the
eight headline benchmarks; $95\%$ CI $[+1.1, +2.1]$, excludes zero).
When more stability is needed, twin-indep at the BACE-frozen
$\lambda{=}300$ removes the next layer of churn at $2\times$ ERM
training cost (matched compute against bagging-$K{=}2$).

\paragraph{Position relative to prior work.}
Cross-sample fragility is the missing axis in the standard uncertainty
toolkit.  Deep ensembles, MC dropout, conformal prediction, and the
group-robustness literature each address a different axis of prediction
uncertainty.  Cross-sample fragility is the one that corresponds to
the deployment quantity practitioners actually care about: whether a
prediction will change at the next retraining.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The fragility definition extends to regression with an MAE or
covered-interval metric in place of class-flip rate, and the
consistency loss becomes an MSE or Huber penalty.  Per-example
fragility is also a candidate signal for influence-function-style
data attribution: examples whose inclusion or exclusion most flips
predictions on others are operationally the most-influential
examples.  An $N$-aware scaling $\lambda_N \propto 1/N$, motivated by
the algorithmic-stability rate, is the natural attempt at removing the
single tuned hyperparameter; whether such a scaling generalises beyond
the dev dataset is an empirical question we do not settle here.
