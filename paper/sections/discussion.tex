\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Churn is reducible with methods the field already has.}
The two procedures that work --- bagging and twin-bootstrap --- need
nothing exotic.  Bagging is a 30-year-old method already implemented
in every chemistry-ML toolkit; on the benchmarks we test it cuts
ERM's $\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ churn down to
$\bagFiveFinalLow\text{--}\bagFiveFinalHigh\%$ at no accuracy cost
and parallelises to ERM wall-clock on $K$ devices
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  Twin-bootstrap, which we propose, requires
two networks trained jointly with a sym-KL consistency loss --- a
modification of standard training that any group running PyTorch can
deploy --- and pushes the rate to
$\twinFinalLow\text{--}\twinFinalHigh\%$ at $2\times$-ERM compute.
Neither method requires a new optimisation framework, an unusual loss
beyond cross-entropy plus KL, or specialised hardware.  The
practical implication: per-prediction stability under retraining is a
column the field can start reporting tomorrow.

\paragraph{A recommended workflow.}
A tiered recipe that practitioners can adopt incrementally on an
existing pipeline.  (i)~Bagging-$K{=}5$ on the deployed predictor,
parallelisable to ERM wall-clock at $5\times$ compute
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  (ii)~Twin-bootstrap at the rule-selected
$\lambda$ when $5\times$ compute is prohibitive; recovers most of the
$K{=}5$ benefit at $2\times$-ERM compute (Figure~\ref{fig:main}).
(iii)~For triage on top of any deployed model, train one extra ERM
bootstrap and route the top-$\reviewFraction\%$-churn predictions
to a reviewer (Figure~\ref{fig:decile}).

\paragraph{Stability is a property of the procedure.}
What sets a model's cross-sample churn is the training procedure that
produced its weights --- the optimiser, the schedule, and especially
the data-resampling protocol.  In the codistillation-vs.\
twin-bootstrap comparison (Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}), a
single hyperparameter on the procedure --- the fraction of data the
two networks share --- separates a setting where churn increases on
DILI ($\overlapPctHi\%$ overlap) from one where churn never increases
on any dataset ($\sim\overlapPctMid\%$).  The same lever, the
consistency weight $\lambda$, takes a different value on a different
architecture, but the rule that selects it is unchanged
(Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}).

\paragraph{Cross-sample churn belongs in the benchmark report.}
AI-for-science benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox,
materials-science) report columns for accuracy, AUROC, and
calibration.  None varies the training dataset; cross-sample churn does, and on every benchmark we
test the per-example flip rate is many times larger than the
aggregate-accuracy drift it lives behind
(Section~\ref{sec:churn-magnitudes}, Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).
Clinical biostatistics already reports per-individual stability
intervals~\citep{Riley2023}; the case for adding a churn column to
scientific-ML benchmarks is the same case --- the magnitudes are
large enough to matter (Section~\ref{sec:churn-magnitudes}).  Once
the column is in the report, bagging and twin-bootstrap reduce it,
and a one-bootstrap triage identifies which predictions will flip
first.
