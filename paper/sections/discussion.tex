\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What practitioners should do.}
For deployed scientific machine learning at $N \lesssim 5000$ from
scratch, run $K$-bootstrap bagging with $K{=}5$ as the default: it
removes roughly half of the per-example retraining churn at a
$5\times$ training cost, with no accuracy regression
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  When stability matters more than compute,
twin-indep at $\lambda{=}300$ removes the next layer of churn at
$2\times$ ERM training cost, matched against bagging-$K{=}2$.

\paragraph{Stability is a property of the training procedure.}
A model's cross-sample churn is not a fact about the trained weights;
it is a fact about the procedure that produced them --- the
optimiser, the schedule, the data-resampling protocol.  The
codistillation-vs.\ twin-indep comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}) makes this concrete: a single
hyperparameter on the procedure --- the fraction of data the two
networks share --- separates a method that catastrophically fails on
TADF from a method that does not.  None of the standard report
columns (accuracy, AUROC, calibration, worst-group accuracy) varies
the dataset; cross-sample fragility does, and it is the column that
tracks what practitioners observe between retrainings on a refreshed
sample.  The clinical-biostatistics
literature~\citep{TODO_riley2023} already reports per-individual
stability intervals; the case for the same in scientific ML
benchmarks rests on the magnitudes we measure
(Section~\ref{sec:measurement}).

\paragraph{Future work.}
Bootstrap-aware data augmentation, subbagging schedules, and
training-set diversity penalties would act on the same axis as
twin-indep; matched-compute comparisons against any of them are open.
An $N$-aware scaling $\lambda_N \propto 1/N$ is the obvious
follow-up for removing the one tuned hyperparameter; we have not
verified it beyond BACE.  The fragility definition extends unchanged
to regression (with an MAE or covered-interval metric in place of
class-flip rate) and to multi-class.
