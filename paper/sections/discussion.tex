\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What practitioners should do.}
For deployed scientific machine learning at $N \lesssim 5000$ from
scratch, run $K$-bootstrap bagging with $K{=}5$ as the default: it
removes roughly half of the per-example retraining churn at a
$5\times$ training cost, with no accuracy regression
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  When stability matters more than compute,
twin-indep at $\lambda{=}300$ removes the next layer of churn at
$2\times$ ERM training cost, matched against bagging-$K{=}2$.

\paragraph{Position relative to prior work.}
Deep ensembles, MC dropout, conformal prediction, and the
group-robustness literature each track a different axis of prediction
uncertainty (parameter variance at fixed data, marginal coverage
under a fixed training set, worst-group accuracy under shift).
Cross-sample fragility tracks the axis that varies under retraining
on a fresh sample of the same population, which is the quantity
practitioners observe between deployments.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The fragility definition extends to regression with an MAE or
covered-interval metric in place of class-flip rate, and the
consistency loss becomes an MSE or Huber penalty.  Per-example
fragility is also a candidate signal for influence-function-style
data attribution: examples whose inclusion or exclusion most flips
predictions on others are operationally the most-influential
examples.  An $N$-aware scaling $\lambda_N \propto 1/N$, motivated by
the algorithmic-stability rate, is the natural attempt at removing the
single tuned hyperparameter; whether such a scaling generalises beyond
the dev dataset is an empirical question we do not settle here.
