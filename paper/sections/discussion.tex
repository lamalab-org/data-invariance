\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Reducing cross-sample prediction fragility.}
The intro's toxicity classifier loses class on
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test molecules between retrainings
on independent bootstraps of the same training population.
Bagging-$K{=}5$ cuts that to $4\text{--}11\%$ at no accuracy cost
($5\times$-ERM compute, Table~\ref{tab:main}).  Twin-bootstrap at
$\lambda{=}300$ ($2\times$-ERM compute) cuts it to $1\text{--}14\%$,
beating matched-compute bagging-$K{=}2$ on $8/9$ datasets and
matching the $K{=}5$ result in mean rank
(Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}).

\paragraph{A recommended workflow.}
The methods admit a tiered recipe that practitioners can adopt
incrementally on top of an existing pipeline.  (i)~For the deployed
predictor, train $K{=}5$ bagged models on independent bootstraps;
this cuts the class-flip rate
$\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ at no accuracy cost and
parallelises across $K$ devices for the same wall-clock as empirical
risk minimisation (ERM).  (ii)~If $5\times$ compute is
prohibitive, run a one-time $\lambda$-sweep on a development dataset
and switch to twin-bootstrap at the rule-selected $\lambda$ (the
$0.02$-tolerance rule selects $\lambda{=}300$ on from-scratch
fingerprint multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), $\lambda{=}10$ on
pretrained backbones); twin-bootstrap recovers most of the
$K{=}5$ benefit at $2{\times}$-ERM compute.  (iii)~For
triage, train one extra ERM bootstrap and rank test predictions by
their per-example fragility; reviewing the top $30\%$ catches
$\triageKtenLow\text{--}\triageKtenHigh\%$ of all flips on every dataset
(Figure~\ref{fig:decile}), at the cost of one extra training run.

\paragraph{Stability is a property of the procedure.}
A model's cross-sample churn is not a fact about the trained
weights; it is a fact about the procedure that produced them ---
the optimiser, the schedule, the data-resampling protocol.  In the
codistillation-vs.\ twin-bootstrap comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}), a single hyperparameter on
the procedure --- the fraction of data the two networks share ---
separates a column that goes positive on DILI ($\overlapPctHi\%$ overlap) from
one that never does ($\sim\overlapPctMid\%$).  The same lever, the consistency
weight $\lambda$, takes a different value on a different architecture
but the rule that selects it is unchanged
(Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}).

\paragraph{Cross-sample fragility belongs in the benchmark report.}
AI-for-science benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox,
materials-science suites) report columns for accuracy, area under
the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC), calibration, and
(sometimes) worst-group accuracy.  None of these varies the training
dataset; cross-sample fragility does.  Two models trained on
independent samples of the same population that disagree on $16\%$
of test predictions while reporting near-identical aggregate
accuracy (within $\sim$$2$\,pp) --- the BACE case
(Figure~\ref{fig:overview}) --- are indistinguishable in the report
column even though the practitioner sees the difference at the next
retraining.  Deep ensembles and MC dropout, the standard
parameter-side uncertainty methods, move argmax churn by $-4.6$ to
$+1.8$\,pp across the \nDatasetsHeadline{} datasets (Table~\ref{tab:main}); they
do not substitute for measurement of sensitivity to the training
sample.  Clinical biostatistics already reports per-individual
stability intervals~\citep{Riley2023}; the case for the same
in scientific ML rests on the magnitudes we measure
(Section~\ref{sec:fragility-magnitudes}).  Aggregate accuracy on a
fixed test set characterises the deployed model; cross-sample
fragility characterises the procedure that will produce the next
one.  A practitioner who refreshes a dataset and retrains sees this
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ flip rate on the molecules that drive next steps:
synthesis queues, lead candidates, virtual-screening hits.  The
measurement protocol surfaces the rate, bagging and twin-bootstrap
reduce it, and a one-bootstrap triage identifies which predictions
will flip first.
