\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the methods buy.}
The intro's toxicity classifier loses class on $8\text{--}17\%$ of
test molecules between retrainings on independent bootstraps of the
same training population.  Bagging-$K{=}5$ cuts that to
$4\text{--}9\%$ at no accuracy cost (Table~\ref{tab:main}); twin-indep
at $\lambda{=}300$ cuts it further to $1\text{--}7\%$ at $2\times$-ERM
compute matched against bagging-$K{=}2$.  A single additional
bootstrap suffices to identify the top decile of fragile predictions,
which captures $68\text{--}94\%$ of the molecules retraining will flip
(Figure~\ref{fig:decile}); a triage workflow that routes only that
decile to a human reviewer is therefore a one-bootstrap operation on
top of an existing pipeline.

\paragraph{Cross-sample fragility belongs alongside accuracy.}
Scientific-ML benchmark reports columns for accuracy, AUROC,
calibration, and (sometimes) worst-group accuracy.  None of these
varies the training dataset; cross-sample fragility does.  Two models
trained on independent samples of the same population that disagree
on $16\%$ of test predictions while reporting identical aggregate
accuracy --- the BACE case (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}) --- are
indistinguishable in the report column even though the practitioner
sees the difference at the next retraining.  Deep ensembles and MC
dropout, the standard parameter-side uncertainty methods, leave
$-0.2$ to $+1.8$pp on argmax churn (Table~\ref{tab:main}); they are
not substitutes for a report column that measures sensitivity to the
training sample.  The clinical-biostatistics literature already
reports per-individual stability intervals~\citep{TODO_riley2023};
the case for the same in scientific ML rests on the magnitudes we
measure (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}).

\paragraph{Stability is a property of the procedure.}
A model's cross-sample churn is not a fact about the trained
weights; it is a fact about the procedure that produced them ---
the optimiser, the schedule, the data-resampling protocol.  The
codistillation-vs.\ twin-indep comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}) makes this concrete: a single
hyperparameter on the procedure --- the fraction of data the two
networks share --- separates a method that catastrophically fails on
TADF from a method that does not.  The same applies across
architectures: at the BACE-MLP-frozen $\lambda{=}300$ twin-indep
over-regularises GIN by $15$pp id-accuracy; the same selection rule
re-applied on BACE-GIN picks $\lambda{=}10$ and recovers the result
($-47.6\%$ argmax churn vs.\ ERM-GIN, Appendix~\ref{app:gin}).  The
selection rule transfers; the numerical $\lambda$ does not.  The
recipe in this paper is tied to small-$N$ from-scratch chemistry
with one $\lambda$ per architecture and binary classification;
regression, multi-class, pretrained backbones, and $N$-aware
$\lambda$-scaling are open
(Section~\ref{sec:scope}).

\paragraph{Two columns in the report.}
Bagging~\citep{TODO_breiman1996} and codistillation
\citep{TODO_anil2018_codistillation} at the right inter-network
overlap already exist; what this paper adds is a measurement
protocol that makes their effect on cross-sample fragility visible,
and a regime --- small-$N$ from-scratch scientific ML --- where the
gap they close dominates the rest of the report.  Aggregate accuracy
on a fixed test set measures the deployed model; cross-sample
fragility measures the procedure that will produce the next one.  On
the eight chemistry benchmarks where we measured both, the second
varies by $8\text{--}17$ percentage points across retrainings while
the first does not move.  Reading only the accuracy column is
reading half the report.
