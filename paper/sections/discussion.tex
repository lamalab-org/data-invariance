\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What practitioners should do.}
For deployed scientific machine learning at $N \lesssim 5000$ from
scratch, run $K$-bootstrap bagging with $K{=}5$ as the default: it
removes roughly half of the per-example retraining churn at a
$5\times$ training cost, with no accuracy regression
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  When stability matters more than compute,
twin-indep at $\lambda{=}300$ removes the next layer of churn at
$2\times$ ERM training cost, matched against bagging-$K{=}2$.

\paragraph{Cross-sample fragility belongs alongside accuracy.}
Two ERM models with identical aggregate accuracy can disagree on a
fifth of the test set.  None of the standard report columns ---
accuracy, AUROC, calibration, ECE, worst-group accuracy --- captures
that disagreement, because each fixes the dataset and varies something
else (architecture, threshold, group).  Cross-sample fragility is
the column that varies the dataset and fixes everything else, and it
is the column that tracks the deployment quantity practitioners
observe between retrainings.  The clinical-biostatistics
literature~\citep{TODO_riley2023} already reports per-individual
stability intervals as a model-development standard; the case for
the same in scientific machine learning is straightforward to make
once the magnitudes ($8\text{--}17\%$ flip rate at deployment-relevant
$N$) are visible.

\paragraph{Stability is a property of the procedure, not the model.}
The deeper consequence is that any method which does not optimise
against the data-resampling axis --- which is most of them --- leaves
that axis on the table.  Twin-indep is one consistency objective that
does; we expect simpler data-side regularisers (subbagging schedules,
bootstrap-aware data augmentation, training-set diversity penalties)
to land on the same axis once it is reported.  An $N$-aware scaling
$\lambda_N \propto 1/N$, motivated by the algorithmic-stability rate
but unverified beyond BACE, is the natural next attempt at removing
the single tuned hyperparameter.  The fragility definition extends
unchanged to regression (MAE or covered-interval metric in place of
class-flip rate) and to multi-class without protocol changes; we
verify neither here.
