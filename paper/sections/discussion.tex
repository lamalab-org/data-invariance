\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Churn is reducible with methods the field already has.}
Both procedures evaluated here are simple modifications of standard
classifier training.  Bagging is the classical estimator
of \citet{Breiman1996}, already available in every chemistry-ML
toolkit; on the benchmarks we test it cuts ERM's
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ churn to
$\bagFiveFinalLow\text{--}\bagFiveFinalHigh\%$ at no accuracy cost
and parallelises to ERM wall-clock on $K$ devices
(Table~\ref{tab:main}).  Twin-bootstrap adds a symmetric-KL
consistency term between two networks trained jointly on independent
bootstraps and reaches
$\twinFinalLow\text{--}\twinFinalHigh\%$ at $2\times$-ERM compute.
Per-prediction stability under retraining is the column scientific-ML
benchmarks have so far overlooked despite its weight on practitioners
who act on individual predictions; the methods that reduce it are
simple modifications of supervised training, not new infrastructure.

\paragraph{The data-resampling protocol is the design lever.}
Cross-sample stability is set less by the model class than by how the
training procedure resamples data between independent runs.  The
codistillation-vs.\ twin-bootstrap comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}) isolates the inter-network
bootstrap overlap as the operative parameter: at $\overlapPctHi\%$
overlap churn increases on DILI; at $\sim\overlapPctMid\%$ churn never
increases on any of the \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry datasets we
tested.  The consistency weight $\lambda$ that controls how strongly
the procedure exploits this overlap takes a different numerical
value on different architectures, but the development-set rule that
selects it transfers unchanged across MLP, GIN, ChemBERTa, and
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50
(Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}).  Cross-sample stability can
therefore be designed in at training time --- without changing the
model class or the deployment pipeline --- by tuning a single
hyperparameter on the development set with the same rule.
