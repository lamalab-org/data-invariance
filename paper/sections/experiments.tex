\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Cross-sample reduction across benchmarks.}
Across the nine chemistry benchmarks (BACE plus eight held-out),
bagging-$K{=}5$ and twin-indep both beat ERM on cross-sample
id-churn with $95\%$ paired-bootstrap CIs that exclude zero
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}).  MC dropout, in
contrast, fails to reduce argmax churn: $\Delta$ ranges $-13\%$ to
$+7\%$ relative to ERM, with no consistent direction.  Training a
single model with dropout and averaging $T{=}20$ stochastic forward
passes does not address the data-resampling axis that bagging and
twin-indep operate on.  At matched compute against bagging-$K{=}2$,
twin-indep wins on $8/9$ datasets (CI excludes zero; the difference
is not significant on DILI, where ERM accuracy is $0.72$ on a
$76$-example test set); against the $5\times$-compute
bagging-$K{=}5$, twin-indep wins on $5/8$ held-out datasets and
trails on three (DILI, Pgp, TADF).  The aggregate Friedman test
rejects equal-ranks at $\chi^2 = 38.4$, $p = 3.1 \times 10^{-7}$;
twin-indep ranks first, indistinguishable from bagging-$K{=}5$ at
less than half the compute (Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}).

\paragraph{Distributional disagreement.}
Argmax churn projects the full prediction distribution onto its mode.
Symmetric KL between two bootstraps distinguishes methods more finely:
bagging-$K{=}5$ reduces it $5\text{--}10\times$ vs.\ ERM, and
twin-indep reduces it by another factor of ${\sim}8$.  Pipelines that
consume the softmax (uncertainty-weighted ranking, candidate scoring)
see a larger gap between the two than the argmax-churn column
reflects.

\paragraph{Top-decile fragility predicts which predictions flip.}
\label{sec:churn-decile}
Per-example fragility, computed across pairs of ERM bootstraps, ranks
test examples by how likely they are to flip class on retraining.
Figure~\ref{fig:decile} bins test examples by fragility decile and
reports the per-decile flip rate.  The bottom-$50\%$-fragility
examples flip at $0\%$ on every dataset we test, deciles $6$--$8$
rise sharply, and the top decile flips at $41\text{--}47\%$.
Predictive entropy from a single ERM model ranks worse on this task
(Appendix~\ref{app:entropy}).  A practitioner can therefore identify
the $10\%$ most fragile predictions from a single additional
retraining --- one extra bootstrap on top of the deployed pipeline.

\paragraph{Per-molecule stabilisation on BACE.}
Figure~\ref{fig:case_study} shows six BACE id-test molecules where
ERM flips class on $\geq 36\%$ of seed pairs and twin-indep flips on
$0\%$ over the same ten retrainings.  Both methods see the same
canonical training pool and test set; the difference is the
consistency loss.

\subsection{Development analysis (BACE)}
\label{sec:dev}

The single hyperparameter, twin-indep $\lambda$, is selected on BACE
by sweeping $\lambda \in \{1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300\}$ and picking the
largest grid point with BACE id-accuracy within $0.02$ of ERM
id-accuracy.  This rule yields $\lambda{=}300$, which we apply
unchanged on every held-out dataset.  Appendix~\ref{app:pareto} gives
the full Pareto curve.
