\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Cross-sample reduction across benchmarks.}
Across the eight chemistry benchmarks where the methods apply (BACE
plus seven held-out; MOF-thermal is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}), bagging-$K{=}5$ and twin-indep both beat
ERM on cross-sample id-churn with $95\%$ paired-bootstrap CIs that
exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}).  MC
dropout, in contrast, leaves argmax churn essentially unchanged
($\Delta$ ranges $-2.2\%$ to $+12.5\%$ relative to ERM, median
$+1\%$): training a single model with dropout and averaging $T{=}20$
stochastic forward passes does not address the data-resampling axis
that bagging and twin-indep operate on.  At matched compute against
bagging-$K{=}2$, twin-indep wins on every dataset (left panel of
Figure~\ref{fig:main}, all CIs exclude zero); against the
$5\times$-compute bagging-$K{=}5$, twin-indep wins on $4/7$ held-out
datasets and trails on three (DILI, Pgp, TADF).  The aggregate
Friedman test rejects equal-ranks at $\chi^2 = 24.34$,
$p = 6.8 \times 10^{-5}$; twin-indep ranks first, indistinguishable
from bagging-$K{=}5$ at less than half the compute
(Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}).

\paragraph{Distributional disagreement.}
Argmax churn projects the full prediction distribution onto its mode.
Symmetric KL between two bootstraps distinguishes methods more finely:
bagging-$K{=}5$ reduces it $5\text{--}10\times$ vs.\ ERM, and
twin-indep reduces it by another order of magnitude.  Pipelines that
consume the softmax (uncertainty-weighted ranking, candidate scoring)
therefore see a larger gap between the two than the argmax-churn
column reflects.

\paragraph{Top-decile fragility predicts which predictions flip.}
A single additional bootstrap is sufficient to identify the $10\%$
most fragile predictions; this top decile contains $68\text{--}94\%$
of the predictions that would flip on retraining
(Figure~\ref{fig:decile}).

\paragraph{Per-molecule stabilisation on BACE.}
Figure~\ref{fig:case_study} shows six BACE id-test molecules where
ERM flips class on $\geq 30\%$ of seed pairs and twin-indep flips on
$0\%$ over the same ten retrainings.  Both methods see the same
canonical training pool and test set; the difference is the
consistency loss.

\subsection{Development analysis (BACE)}
\label{sec:dev}

The single hyperparameter, twin-indep $\lambda$, is selected on BACE
by sweeping $\lambda \in \{1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300\}$ and picking the
largest grid point with BACE id-accuracy within $0.02$ of ERM
id-accuracy.  This rule yields $\lambda{=}300$, which we apply
unchanged on every held-out dataset.  Appendix~\ref{app:pareto} gives
the full Pareto curve.
