\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Cross-sample reduction across benchmarks.}
Across the \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks (BACE plus
\nDatasetsHeldout{} held-out), bagging-$K{=}5$ and twin-bootstrap both
beat ERM on cross-sample id-churn with $95\%$ paired-bootstrap CIs
that exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}).  MC
dropout, in contrast, fails to reduce argmax churn: $\Delta$ ranges
$\mcdLow\%$ to $\mcdHigh\%$ relative to ERM, with no consistent
direction.  Training a single model with dropout and averaging
$T{=}20$ stochastic forward passes does not address the
data-resampling axis that bagging and twin-bootstrap operate on.  At
matched compute against bagging-$K{=}2$, twin-bootstrap wins on $8/9$
datasets (CI excludes zero; the difference is not significant on
DILI, where ERM accuracy is $0.72$ on a $76$-example test set);
against the $5\times$-compute bagging-$K{=}5$, twin-bootstrap wins on
$5/8$ held-out datasets and trails on three (DILI, Pgp, TADF).  The
aggregate Friedman test rejects equal-ranks at
$\chi^2 = \friedmanChi$, $p = \friedmanP$; twin-bootstrap ranks
first, indistinguishable from bagging-$K{=}5$ at less than half the
compute (Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}).

\paragraph{Distributional disagreement.}
Argmax churn projects the full prediction distribution onto its mode.
Symmetric KL between two bootstraps distinguishes methods more finely:
bagging-$K{=}5$ reduces it
$\symKLBagFoldLow\text{--}\symKLBagFoldHigh\times$ vs.\ ERM, and
twin-bootstrap reduces it by another factor of
${\sim}\symKLTwinAdditionalFoldMedian$ (per-dataset paired CIs in
Table~\ref{tab:distributional}, Appendix~\ref{app:distributional}).  Pipelines that consume the
softmax (uncertainty-weighted ranking, candidate scoring) see a
larger gap between the two than the argmax-churn column reflects.

\paragraph{Fragility-ranked triage captures most flips at low review cost.}
\label{sec:churn-decile}
Per-example fragility, computed across pairs of ERM bootstraps, ranks
test examples by how likely they are to flip class on retraining.
Figure~\ref{fig:decile} reports the cumulative fraction of total
flip-mass captured if the practitioner sorts predictions by
fragility and reviews the top fraction.  Reviewing the top $30\%$
captures $\triageKtenLow\text{--}\triageKtenHigh\%$ of all flips
when fragility is scored from all $10$ bootstraps; with a single
extra retraining ($K{=}2$, the one-bootstrap practitioner workflow)
the top-$30\%$ recall is
$\triageKtwoLow\text{--}\triageKtwoHigh\%$, within
$\triageKtwoToKtenGapLow\text{--}\triageKtwoToKtenGapHigh$\,pp
of the $K{=}10$ gold standard on every dataset
(Appendix~\ref{app:convergence}).  Predictive entropy from a single
ERM model ranks worse on this task: fragility captures
$\fragTopLow\text{--}\fragTopHigh\%$ of flips in its top decile vs.\
$\entropyTopLow\text{--}\entropyTopHigh\%$ for entropy (per-dataset
gap $\entropyGapMin\text{--}\entropyGapMax$\,pp,
Appendix~\ref{app:entropy}).
A practitioner can therefore identify
the $10\%$ most fragile predictions from a single additional
retraining --- one extra bootstrap on top of the deployed pipeline.

\subsection{The recipe generalises across architectures and tasks}
\label{sec:generalisation}

The main result on small-$N$ from-scratch MLPs holds across three
other architectures and on regression. The numerical value of
$\lambda$ is architecture-specific; the rule that selects it is not.

\paragraph{Pretrained backbones.}
A pretrained backbone is an implicit data prior; the from-scratch
$\lambda$ is too strong for what the backbone already encodes.  At
$\lambda{=}300$ (the BACE-MLP-frozen value), twin-bootstrap collapses
accuracy by $\waterbirdsAccCollapse$pp on Waterbirds
(ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50) and by
$\chembertaAccDropLow\text{--}\chembertaAccDropHigh$pp on
ChemBERTa-77M-MTR fine-tuned on six chemistry datasets, increasing
argmax churn on five of six ChemBERTa datasets.  Re-applying the
same $0.02$-tolerance selection rule on each backbone's development
set picks $\lambda{=}10$: on BACE-ChemBERTa this preserves accuracy
(within $2$pp of ERM) and cuts churn
$\chembertaCutLow\text{--}\chembertaCutHigh\%$ on every ChemBERTa
dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}); on Waterbirds it
preserves accuracy (within $1$pp of ERM) and cuts churn
$\waterbirdsLamTenCut\%$
($\waterbirdsLamTenDeltaPP$pp [$\waterbirdsLamTenDeltaLo$,
$\waterbirdsLamTenDeltaHi$], Appendix~\ref{app:waterbirds_lambda}).
Bagging is independent of $\lambda$ and works on every backbone we
tested.

\paragraph{Graph networks.}
On a non-fingerprint architecture (a $3$-layer GIN on RDKit graphs of
BACE), bagging-$K{=}5$ transfers cleanly: it cuts argmax churn by
$\ginBagCutLamThreeHundred\%$ ($\ginBagDeltaPP$pp [$\ginBagDeltaLo$,
$\ginBagDeltaHi$]) and improves id-accuracy by $\ginBagAccGain$pp.
Twin-bootstrap at $\lambda{=}300$ drops id-accuracy by
$\ginAccDropLamThreeHundred$pp and is rejected by the same
accuracy-tolerance rule that selected $\lambda{=}300$ on the MLP;
re-running the rule on BACE-GIN selects $\lambda{=}\ginRuleLam$,
which cuts argmax churn $\ginCutLamTen\%$ at no accuracy cost
(Appendix~\ref{app:gin}).

\paragraph{Regression.}
On three regression benchmarks (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity;
Appendix~\ref{app:regression}) the methods extend to continuous
targets and rank the same way as on classification.  At matched
$2\times$-ERM compute, twin-bootstrap cuts prediction-difference
fragility $\regTwinLow\text{--}\regTwinHigh\%$ vs.\ ERM while
bagging-$K{=}2$ cuts $\regBagTwoLow\text{--}\regBagTwoHigh\%$
(twin-bootstrap wins on every dataset).  Bagging-$K{=}5$ at
$5\times$-ERM compute is strongest at
$\regBagFiveLow\text{--}\regBagFiveHigh\%$.
