\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Cross-sample reduction across benchmarks.}
Across the eight held-out chemistry benchmarks, every method beats ERM
on cross-sample id-churn with $95\%$ paired-bootstrap CIs that
exclude zero.  Bagging $K{=}5$ reduces mean id-churn from
$10.8\%$ to $5.5\%$ at no accuracy cost.  Twin-indep at the
BACE-frozen $\lambda{=}300$ wins on every dataset against
compute-matched bagging $K{=}2$ ($\Delta = -1.5$ to $-7.7$pp,
all CIs exclude zero); against the $5\times$-compute Bagging $K{=}5$,
twin-indep wins on $4/7$ datasets and trails on the remaining
$3$ (DILI, Pgp, TADF).

Every comparison in Table~\ref{tab:main} is paired across the
${10 \choose 2} = 45$ seed pairs; the $95\%$ confidence intervals
shown come from $10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the seed-pair
distribution.  Section~\ref{sec:dev} reports the development analysis

\paragraph{Compute-matched comparison: twin-indep vs.\ bagging $K{=}2$.}
Both methods train two networks.  At matched compute, twin-indep
beats bagging $K{=}2$ on cross-sample id-churn on all four
small-$N$ MoleculeNet/MOF datasets (paired
$\Delta = -0.015$ to $-0.077$, all $95\%$ CIs exclude $0$).  The
consistency loss therefore contributes beyond the data-resampling
already in bagging.

\paragraph{Argmax churn vs.\ distributional disagreement.}
Argmax churn is a coarse projection of the full prediction
distribution; the distributional sym-KL between two bootstraps is the
sharper signal.  Bagging $K{=}5$ reduces sym-KL by $5\text{--}10\times$;
twin-indep reduces it by an additional order of magnitude, often to
within numerical noise.  In a setting where downstream decisions use
the full softmax (e.g.\ uncertainty-weighted ranking of synthesis
candidates), the gap between bagging and twin-indep is much larger
than the argmax-churn delta suggests.

\paragraph{Top-decile fragility flagging.}
Combining the deployed model with a single additional bootstrap suffices
to identify the $10\%$ most fragile predictions, which contain
$68\text{--}94\%$ of the test predictions that would flip on
retraining (Figure~\ref{fig:decile}).  This is a deployment
recipe: compute fragility, route the top decile for re-review.

\paragraph{Per-molecule stabilisation (BACE case study).}
The aggregate reductions resolve at the per-molecule level
(Figure~\ref{fig:case_study}).  We pick six BACE id-test molecules
where ERM flips class on $\geq 30\%$ of seed pairs and twin-indep
flips on $0\%$.  These are drug-discovery candidates that the
deployed pipeline would route to synthesis differently across
retrainings if ERM is used --- and consistently to the same arm
under twin-indep.  The same canonical training pool was used for
both methods; the difference is the consistency loss alone.

\subsection{Development analysis (BACE)}
\label{sec:dev}

The single hyperparameter, twin-indep $\lambda$, is selected on BACE
by sweeping $\lambda \in \{1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300\}$ and picking the
largest grid point with BACE id-accuracy within $0.02$ of ERM
id-accuracy.  This rule yields $\lambda{=}300$, which we apply
unchanged on every held-out dataset.  Appendix~\ref{app:pareto} gives
the full Pareto curve.
