\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Cross-sample reduction across benchmarks.}
Across the seven chemistry benchmarks (BACE plus six held-out), every
method beats ERM on cross-sample id-churn with $95\%$ paired-bootstrap
CIs that exclude zero (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}).
At matched compute against bagging-$K{=}2$, twin-indep wins on every
dataset (left panel of Figure~\ref{fig:main}, all CIs exclude zero);
against the $5\times$-compute bagging-$K{=}5$, twin-indep wins on
$3/6$ held-out datasets and trails on three (DILI, Pgp, TADF).
The aggregate Friedman test rejects equal-ranks at $\chi^2 = 24.34$,
$p = 6.8 \times 10^{-5}$; twin-indep ranks first, indistinguishable
from bagging-$K{=}5$ at less than half the compute
(Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}).

Every comparison in Table~\ref{tab:main} is paired across the
${10 \choose 2} = 45$ seed pairs; the $95\%$ confidence intervals
shown come from $10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the seed-pair
distribution.  Section~\ref{sec:dev} reports the development analysis
on BACE (Pareto curve, Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}).

\paragraph{Argmax churn vs.\ distributional disagreement.}
Argmax churn projects the full prediction distribution onto the
predicted class.  The distributional sym-KL between two bootstraps
distinguishes methods more finely: bagging $K{=}5$ reduces it
$5\text{--}10\times$ vs.\ ERM, and twin-indep reduces it by an
additional order of magnitude on top.  Downstream pipelines that
consume the softmax (e.g.\ uncertainty-weighted ranking) therefore
see a larger gap between bagging and twin-indep than the argmax-churn
column suggests.

\paragraph{Top-decile fragility flagging.}
Combining the deployed model with a single additional bootstrap suffices
to identify the $10\%$ most fragile predictions, which contain
$68\text{--}94\%$ of the test predictions that would flip on
retraining (Figure~\ref{fig:decile}).  This is a deployment
recipe: compute fragility, route the top decile for re-review.

\paragraph{Per-molecule stabilisation (BACE case study).}
The aggregate reductions resolve at the per-molecule level
(Figure~\ref{fig:case_study}).  We pick six BACE id-test molecules
where ERM flips class on at least $30\%$ of seed pairs and
twin-indep flips on $0\%$ across the same ten retrainings; the
canonical training pool and test set are identical for both methods,
so the difference is the consistency loss alone.

\subsection{Development analysis (BACE)}
\label{sec:dev}

The single hyperparameter, twin-indep $\lambda$, is selected on BACE
by sweeping $\lambda \in \{1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300\}$ and picking the
largest grid point with BACE id-accuracy within $0.02$ of ERM
id-accuracy.  This rule yields $\lambda{=}300$, which we apply
unchanged on every held-out dataset.  Appendix~\ref{app:pareto} gives
the full Pareto curve.
