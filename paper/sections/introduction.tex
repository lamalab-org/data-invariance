A toxicity classifier is retrained on a new batch of assay results.
Aggregate accuracy is unchanged.  The class label flips on
$8\text{--}22\%$ of test molecules --- some already in synthesis
queues (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  We call this \emph{cross-sample
prediction fragility}: the fraction of test predictions that change
class between two models trained on independent samples of the same
training population.

Cross-sample fragility is the population-level analogue of
\emph{prediction churn}~\citep{TODO_milani2016, TODO_bhojanapalli2021,
TODO_jiang2022}: prior work measures churn between a new model and
a deployed incumbent at web scale, where the incumbent is fixed.
Scientific machine learning often retrains from scratch on a few
hundred to a few thousand examples and has no incumbent; what
varies between retrainings is the sample drawn from the training
population.  Cross-sample fragility is a distinct axis of predictive
uncertainty: deep ensembles, MC dropout, and conformal prediction
all sample over model parameters at fixed training data; cross-sample
fragility samples over draws of the training data itself.  Across
our nine chemistry benchmarks the parameter-side methods move the
class-flip rate by $-21\%$ to $+12.5\%$ relative to ERM, with no
consistent direction.  Methods that vary the data work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item We define cross-sample prediction fragility, the
        population-level analogue of prediction churn, and give a
        measurement protocol for the small-$N$ from-scratch regime
        where deployment-loop churn measurement does not apply.
  \item On nine chemistry benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox,
        materials science) fragility flips $8\text{--}22\%$ of test
        predictions across retrainings --- many times larger than
        the parameter-variance axis deep ensembles and MC dropout
        capture (which leave $-13\%$ to $+7\%$ relative on
        the class-flip rate).
  \item $K$-bootstrap bagging~\citep{TODO_breiman1996} reduces argmax
        churn $40\text{--}54\%$ on every held-out dataset, paired
        bootstrap CI excluding zero, with no accuracy regression.
        Twin-bootstrap --- the codistillation
        family~\citep{TODO_anil2018_codistillation} at $\sim$$40\%$
        inter-network bootstrap overlap --- cuts the class-flip rate by a
        further median $60\%$ (range $16\text{--}92\%$) at matched
        compute against bagging-$K{=}2$, at a frozen $\lambda{=}300$
        selected on BACE alone, and reduces distributional
        disagreement by another factor of ${\sim}8$.  Across three
        operating points on the codistillation family ($0\%$,
        $\sim$$40\%$, $\sim$$63\%$ inter-network overlap), only
        $\sim$$40\%$ holds accuracy on every dataset
        (Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}).
  \item A one-bootstrap triage workflow: per-example fragility from
        a single additional retraining captures $68\text{--}94\%$ of
        the test predictions retraining will flip in its top decile,
        letting practitioners route only that decile to a human
        reviewer at the cost of one extra training run.
  \item The recipe is portable.  A single rule for picking
        $\lambda$ on the development dataset produces a working value
        on every architecture we tested --- the headline MLP, a
        from-scratch GIN (Appendix~\ref{app:gin}), ChemBERTa
        fine-tuned on six chemistry benchmarks
        (Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}), and ImageNet-pretrained
        ResNet-50 fine-tuned on Waterbirds
        (Appendix~\ref{app:waterbirds_lambda}) --- though the value
        differs by architecture.  Bagging requires no tuning and
        works on every architecture and backbone we tested.
\end{itemize}
