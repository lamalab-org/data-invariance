A toxicity classifier is retrained on a new batch of assay results.
Aggregate accuracy moves by $\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$ percentage
points.  The class label flips on
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test molecules --- some already in
synthesis queues (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  We call this \emph{cross-sample
prediction fragility}: the fraction of test predictions that change
class between two models trained on independent samples of the same
training population.

Chemistry models are designed to be invariant to atomic permutation,
rotation, and translation by construction --- $E(3)$-equivariant
message passing, permutation-equivariant aggregators, and explicit
symmetry baking are standard.  \emph{Sample invariance} ---
invariance to which independent draw of the training population the
model was fit on --- is the analogue on the data axis, and it is
the invariance scientific ML has not been measuring.  A practitioner
who refreshes a screening set, retrains, and reroutes synthesis based
on the new top predictions confronts the violation of this invariance
on every dataset update; cross-sample fragility quantifies it.

Cross-sample fragility is the population-level analogue of
\emph{prediction churn}~\citep{launch_and_iterate, bhojanapalli2021reproducibility,
jiang2022}: prior work measures churn between a new model and a
deployed incumbent at web scale, where the incumbent is fixed.
Scientific machine learning often retrains from scratch on a few
hundred to a few thousand examples and has no incumbent; what varies
between retrainings is the sample drawn from the training population.
The quantity is the data-sampling slice of epistemic uncertainty,
complementary to the parameter-sampling deep
ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple} and Monte Carlo (MC)
dropout~\citep{gal2015dropout} capture.  Across our \nDatasetsHeadline{}
chemistry benchmarks the parameter-side methods move the class-flip rate
by $\paramSideLow\%$ to $\paramSideHigh\%$ relative to empirical risk
minimisation (ERM), with no consistent direction; methods that vary
the data work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Measurement protocol.}  We define
        \emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}, the population-level
        analogue of prediction churn, and give a measurement protocol
        for the small-$N$ from-scratch regime where deployment-loop
        churn measurement does not apply.
  \item \textbf{Magnitudes.}  On \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks
        (MoleculeNet, Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC) ADME and Tox,
        materials-science) fragility flips
        $\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test predictions across
        retrainings --- many times larger than the parameter-variance
        axis deep ensembles and MC dropout capture (which leave
        $\paramSideLow\%$ to $\paramSideHigh\%$ relative on the
        class-flip rate, no consistent direction).
  \item \textbf{Bagging as a free-lunch baseline.}
        $K$-bootstrap bagging~\citep{Breiman1996} reduces the
        class-flip rate $\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ on every
        held-out dataset, paired bootstrap CI excluding zero, with no
        accuracy regression.
  \item \textbf{Twin-bootstrap.}  Two networks trained on independent
        bootstraps with a soft sym-KL consistency loss between their
        predictions --- the codistillation
        family~\citep{anil2018large} at $\sim\overlapPctMid\%$
        inter-network bootstrap overlap.  It cuts the class-flip rate
        by a further median $\twinMedianReduction\%$ (range
        $\twinLow\text{--}\twinHigh\%$) at matched $2\times$-ERM
        compute against bagging-$K{=}2$, and reduces distributional
        disagreement by another factor of
        ${\sim}\symKLTwinAdditionalFoldMedian$.  Among three overlap
        points ($\overlapPctLo\%$, $\sim\overlapPctMid\%$,
        $\overlapPctHi\%$), $\sim\overlapPctMid\%$ is the only one
        that reduces churn on every dataset with accuracy collapse on
        at most one shared difficult dataset
        (Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}).
  \item \textbf{One-bootstrap triage.}  Sorting test predictions by
        per-example fragility from a single additional retraining and
        reviewing the top $30\%$ captures
        $\triageKtenLow\text{--}\triageKtenHigh\%$ of the
        retraining-induced flips on every dataset, at the cost of one
        extra training run.
  \item \textbf{The recipe is portable.}  A single rule for picking
        $\lambda$ on the development dataset produces a working value
        on every architecture we tested --- the headline MLP, a
        from-scratch GIN (Appendix~\ref{app:gin}), ChemBERTa
        fine-tuned on six chemistry benchmarks
        (Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}), and ImageNet-pretrained
        ResNet-50 fine-tuned on Waterbirds
        (Appendix~\ref{app:waterbirds_lambda}) --- though the value
        differs by architecture.  Bagging requires no tuning and
        works on every architecture and backbone we tested.
\end{itemize}
