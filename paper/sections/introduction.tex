\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A toxicity classifier is retrained on a new batch of assay results.
Aggregate accuracy moves by $\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$ percentage
points.  The class label flips on
$\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test molecules --- some already in
synthesis queues (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  We call this \emph{cross-sample
prediction churn}: the fraction of test predictions that change
class between two models trained on independent samples of the same
training population.

Chemistry models are designed to be invariant to atomic permutation,
rotation, and translation; $E(3)$-equivariant message passing and
permutation-equivariant aggregators are standard.  \emph{Sample invariance} --- invariance to which independent
draw of the training population the model was fit on --- is the
analogue on the data axis, but is not routinely measured in scientific
ML.  A practitioner who refreshes a screening set, retrains, and
reroutes synthesis based on the new top predictions confronts the
violation on every dataset update; cross-sample churn quantifies
it.

Cross-sample churn is the population-level analogue of
\emph{prediction churn}~\citep{launch_and_iterate, bhojanapalli2021reproducibility,
jiang2022}.  Prior work measures churn between a new model and a
deployed incumbent at web scale, where the incumbent is fixed.
Scientific machine learning often retrains from scratch on a few
hundred to a few thousand examples and has no incumbent; what varies
between retrainings is the sample drawn from the training population.

This is the data-sampling slice of epistemic uncertainty,
complementary to the parameter-sampling that deep
ensembles~\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple} and Monte Carlo (MC)
dropout~\citep{gal2015dropout} capture.  Across our
\nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks, deep ensembles and MC
dropout shift the class-flip rate by $\paramSideLow\%$ to
$\paramSideHigh\%$ relative to empirical risk minimisation (ERM),
with no consistent sign.  These methods marginalise over network
weights at fixed bootstrap (deep ensembles, MC dropout) or smooth
one optimisation trajectory (SWA); the data-sampling axis is held
constant, so the variance they reduce is orthogonal to churn.
Methods that vary the data --- bagging and twin-bootstrap --- do
better.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Measurement protocol.}  We define
        \emph{cross-sample prediction churn}, the population-level
        analogue of prediction churn for the small-$N$ from-scratch
        regime where the deployment-loop churn measurement does not
        apply.
  \item \textbf{Magnitudes.}  On \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry
        benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME and Tox, materials-science)
        churn flips $\churnMin\text{--}\churnMax\%$ of test
        predictions across retrainings; deep ensembles, MC dropout,
        and SWA do not consistently reduce it
        (Section~\ref{sec:churn-magnitudes},
        Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes}).
  \item \textbf{Bagging is a free-lunch reducer.}  $K$-bootstrap
        bagging~\citep{Breiman1996} cuts churn
        $\bagFiveLow\text{--}\bagFiveHigh\%$ on every held-out
        dataset with no accuracy regression
        (Table~\ref{tab:main}).
  \item \textbf{Twin-bootstrap and one-bootstrap triage.}
        Two networks trained on independent bootstraps with a sym-KL
        consistency loss --- the codistillation family at
        $\sim\overlapPctMid\%$ inter-network bootstrap overlap.  At
        matched $2\times$-ERM compute against bagging-$K{=}2$,
        twin-bootstrap cuts churn a further median
        $\twinFurtherMedianReductionVsBagTwo\%$ beyond bagging
        (Table~\ref{tab:main}, Figure~\ref{fig:main}); sorting test
        predictions by per-example churn from one extra
        retraining captures
        $\triageKtenLow\text{--}\triageKtenHigh\%$ of all flips on
        every dataset (Section~\ref{sec:churn-decile}).
\end{itemize}
