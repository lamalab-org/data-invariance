A toxicity classifier is retrained after a new batch of assay results
lands.  Aggregate accuracy is unchanged, yet predictions flip class
for $8\text{--}17\%$ of candidate molecules---some already in
synthesis queues (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  We call this
\emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}: the fraction of test
predictions that change class between two models trained on
independent bootstraps of the same population.

Chemistry machine learning has built architectures around invariances
--- to atom indexing, rotation, conformational degrees of freedom
--- and architectures that respect them are now standard.  A further
invariance has gone unspoken: that a model's prediction on a given
molecule should not depend on which subset of the available data the
model trained on.  In a deployment-driven domain where training sets
evolve continuously, this \emph{cross-sample invariance} governs
whether a deployed prediction survives the next retraining.

Cross-sample fragility is the population-level analogue of the
deployment ``prediction churn'' studied
by~\citet{TODO_milani2016, TODO_bhojanapalli2021, TODO_jiang2022} and
of the bootstrap-stability-interval program developed in clinical
biostatistics by~\citet{TODO_riley2023}; we extend that thread to the
small-$N$ from-scratch setting that scientific machine learning
inhabits.  It is motivated theoretically by algorithmic
stability~\citep{TODO_bousquet2002}, and missed by the standard
uncertainty toolkit: deep ensembles vary model parameters at fixed
data and remove only ${\sim}2$pp of churn on average.  We measure
cross-sample fragility on eight standard chemistry benchmarks (BACE,
BBBP, three materials-science datasets, three TDC ADME/Tox
benchmarks), then introduce two methods that reduce it under a strict
no-test-tuning protocol: $K$-bootstrap bagging at no accuracy cost,
and a twin-network bootstrap-consistency objective at matched
compute.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Cross-sample fragility as a measurement.}  We
        re-target the prediction-churn measurement of
        \citet{TODO_milani2016, TODO_bhojanapalli2021, TODO_jiang2022}
        from deployment-loop churn (a new model regressed against an
        incumbent on web-scale data) to population-level churn between
        two bootstraps of the same training pool, in the small-$N$
        from-scratch regime where no incumbent model exists.  On eight
        chemistry benchmarks, $8\text{--}17\%$ of test predictions
        flip across retrainings --- an order of magnitude larger than
        the parameter-variance axis that deep ensembles capture.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap bagging on the data axis.}  Bagging at
        $K{=}5$~\citep{TODO_breiman1996} is the right intervention
        when the deliverable is per-example stability rather than
        accuracy.  Under a no-test-tuning protocol it reduces argmax
        churn $47\text{--}49\%$ on every held-out dataset
        (paired-bootstrap CI excludes zero on every dataset; mean
        accuracy delta $+1.6$pp [$+1.1, +2.1$]).
  \item \textbf{Twin-indep: codistillation at the right overlap.}
        Twin-indep is the codistillation
        family~\citep{TODO_anil2018_codistillation} at $\sim$$40\%$
        inter-network bootstrap overlap rather than the disjoint-shard
        $0\%$ end of the same axis.  At a frozen
        $\lambda$ selected on a single development dataset (BACE,
        $\lambda{=}300$), it reduces argmax churn $69\text{--}72\%$
        at matched compute against bagging-$K{=}2$ and distributional
        disagreement by roughly an additional order of magnitude.
        On a 10-dataset comparison, twin-indep is the only operating
        point on the codistillation family with no failure case
        (Appendix~\ref{app:overlap_spectrum}).
  \item \textbf{Per-example fragility from one additional bootstrap}
        captures $68\text{--}94\%$ of the predictions that retraining
        will flip in its top decile, giving a deployment-time triage
        signal at the cost of one extra model.
  \item \textbf{Regime characterisation.}  The methods apply in the
        small-$N$ from-scratch setting; on pretrained-backbone
        benchmarks (Waterbirds) the consistency loss over-regularises
        features the backbone already encodes ($-27$pp accuracy at
        the chemistry-tuned $\lambda$); on small-$N$ datasets where
        ERM accuracy is close to majority-class (MOF-thermal),
        consistency methods collapse to the constant predictor.
        Bagging extends across all settings tested.
\end{itemize}
