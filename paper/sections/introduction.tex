A toxicity classifier is retrained after a new batch of assay results
lands.  Aggregate accuracy is unchanged, yet predictions flip class
for $8\text{--}17\%$ of candidate molecules---some already in
synthesis queues (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}).  We call this
\emph{cross-sample prediction fragility}: the fraction of test
predictions that change class between two models trained on
independent bootstraps of the same population.

Chemistry machine learning has built architectures around invariances
--- to atom indexing, rotation, conformational degrees of freedom
--- and architectures that respect them are now standard.  A further
invariance has gone unspoken: that a model's prediction on a given
molecule should not depend on which subset of the available data the
model trained on.  In a deployment-driven domain where training sets
evolve continuously, this \emph{cross-sample invariance} is plausibly
the property that most directly governs whether a deployed prediction
can be trusted at the next retraining.

Cross-sample fragility is the population-level analogue of the
deployment ``prediction churn'' studied
by~\citet{TODO_milani2016, TODO_bhojanapalli2021, TODO_jiang2022} and
of the bootstrap-stability-interval program developed in clinical
biostatistics by~\citet{TODO_riley2023}; we extend that thread to the
small-$N$ from-scratch setting that scientific machine learning
inhabits.  It is motivated theoretically by algorithmic
stability~\citep{TODO_bousquet2002}, and missed by the standard
uncertainty toolkit: deep ensembles vary model parameters at fixed
data and remove only ${\sim}2$pp of churn on average.  We measure
cross-sample fragility on eight standard chemistry benchmarks (BACE,
BBBP, three materials-science datasets, three TDC ADME/Tox
benchmarks), then introduce two methods that reduce it under a strict
no-test-tuning protocol: $K$-bootstrap bagging at no accuracy cost,
and a twin-network bootstrap-consistency objective at matched
compute.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Population-level prediction fragility for small-$N$
        scientific ML.}  We bring the deployment-driven prediction-churn
        literature~\citep{TODO_milani2016, TODO_bhojanapalli2021,
        TODO_jiang2022} and the clinical-biostatistics
        stability-interval program~\citep{TODO_riley2023} into the
        small-$N$ from-scratch regime that scientific ML inhabits.
        Cross-sample fragility, measured between two models trained on
        independent bootstraps of the same population, is a measurable
        analogue of uniform algorithmic
        stability~\citep{TODO_bousquet2002}.  On eight standard chemistry
        benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME/Tox, materials science),
        $8\text{--}17\%$ of test predictions flip across retrainings, an
        order of magnitude larger than deep-ensemble disagreement; deep
        ensembles remove only ${\sim}2$pp of churn.
  \item \textbf{Two methods} under a strict no-test-tuning protocol;
        hyperparameters frozen on a single development dataset (BACE)
        and applied unchanged to every held-out benchmark.
        $K$-bootstrap bagging reduces argmax churn $47\text{--}49\%$ at
        zero accuracy cost (mean $+1.6$pp; $95\%$ CI $[+1.1, +2.1]$).
        \textbf{Twin-indep} --- two networks on independent bootstraps
        with a sym-KL consistency penalty between heads --- reduces
        argmax churn $69\text{--}72\%$ at matched compute against
        bagging-$K{=}2$, and distributional sym-KL by roughly an
        additional order of magnitude beyond bagging.  The construction
        shares mechanics with codistillation~\citep{TODO_anil2018_codistillation};
        we discuss the differences in motivation, regime and protocol in
        Section~\ref{sec:related}.
  \item \textbf{Per-example fragility from one additional bootstrap}
        captures $68\text{--}94\%$ of the predictions that retraining
        will flip in its top decile, giving a deployment-time triage at
        the cost of one extra model.
  \item \textbf{Regime characterisation.}  Twin-indep applies in the
        small-$N$ from-scratch setting that scientific machine learning
        inhabits.  On pretrained-backbone benchmarks (Waterbirds) the
        consistency loss over-regularises features the backbone already
        encodes ($-27$pp accuracy at the chemistry-tuned $\lambda$);
        bagging extends across all settings tested.  Every reported
        delta carries a paired-bootstrap $95\%$ confidence interval over
        seed pairs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contribution type (NeurIPS 2026).}
Use-Inspired.  The methods are designed for and evaluated on a specific
