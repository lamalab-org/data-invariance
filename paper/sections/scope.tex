\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Pretrained backbones (Waterbirds).}
We test the BACE-frozen recipe on Waterbirds, a vision benchmark with
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50.  Bagging $K{=}5$ improves both
accuracy ($+2$pp) and cross-sample churn ($-51\%$ relative).
Twin-indep at the same $\lambda{=}300$ collapses accuracy by $27$pp
while still reducing churn (Table~\ref{tab:waterbirds}).
Reducing $\lambda$ to a principled scaled value $\lambda_N
= \lambda_{\mathrm{dev}} \cdot N_{\mathrm{dev}}/N \approx 60$ recovers
some accuracy but does not return to the Pareto frontier of bagging.

\paragraph{Why the consistency loss interferes with finetuning.}
A pretrained backbone is itself an implicit data prior: ImageNet-scale
training has already collapsed most cross-sample variation in the
learned features.  Our consistency loss imposes the same prior
\emph{again}, on top of finetuning, and the two pull the model toward
an over-averaged solution.  This is a feature: cross-sample fragility
is largest precisely where there is no pretrained prior to anchor
predictions, which is where the consistency loss is most needed.
Bagging adds no such second prior and so extends to the
pretrained-backbone setting unchanged.

\paragraph{Class-proportion shift (MOFs).}
On the two MOF datasets the OOD test split has flipped class
proportions relative to the training distribution.  Both bagging and
twin-indep reduce cross-sample churn, but accuracy on this OOD split
suffers because the methods anchor to the training prior more strongly
than ERM does.  This is a known limitation of any prior-imposing
method under prior shift; conformal-prediction-style recalibration is
complementary.

\paragraph{Class of learners.}
Our results are for binary classification with cross-entropy training.
The fragility definition extends to multi-class and (with a metric
substitution) to regression; we leave the regression extension to
future work.

\paragraph{Architecture and featurisation.}
The chemistry experiments use Morgan fingerprints (radius $2$, $2048$
bits) and a $256$-unit MLP trained from scratch---the canonical
small-$N$ tabular pipeline, and the regime where pretrained models do
not yet help reliably.  D-MPNN~\citep{TODO_yang2019_dmpnn} and
ChemBERTa~\citep{TODO_chithrananda2020_chemberta} would be natural
architectures to test next.  We expect partial transfer at small $N$
and a Waterbirds-like collapse of the consistency loss under heavy
pretraining (Section~\ref{sec:scope}); fragility magnitudes
themselves should remain measurable and within the same order.

\paragraph{Twin head count.}
We report twin-indep with $K{=}2$.  Larger $K$ extends mechanically by
all-pairs sym-KL consistency and would interpolate between bagging-$K$
and twin-indep, but we do not study the trade-off here.

\paragraph{Two protocol choices we did not vary.}
The reported numbers depend on a single canonical seed (\texttt{99})
that fixes test-set construction across all training runs.  We did not
re-run the full sweep at a different canonical seed; canonical-seed
sensitivity, if present, would shift absolute fragility magnitudes
without changing method ordering.  The development hyperparameter
$\lambda$ was selected on BACE alone, then frozen.  Cross-validating
the development rule across multiple dev datasets is a natural
robustness check we leave to follow-up; on the dev curve itself, the
selected $\lambda$ sits at the extreme of the feasible interval, which
suggests the rule is not a knife-edge.

\paragraph{Multi-class.}
The fragility definition reduces to argmax disagreement and a
multi-class symmetric KL with no protocol changes; we have not run
multi-class experiments and so do not claim that the magnitudes
reported here transfer.

\paragraph{Compute used.}
Small-$N$ chemistry experiments (BACE, BBBP, TADF, MOFs, TDC ADME/Tox
benchmarks) run end-to-end on a single laptop CPU; the total compute
budget for the small-$N$ portion of the paper is approximately $50$
CPU-hours.  Waterbirds (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) runs on a single
A100 GPU with a budget of approximately $6$ GPU-hours total.  Each
\texttt{cross\_sample\_train.py} run prints reproducibility metadata
(canonical seed, train seed, mode, $\lambda$).  All trained
predictions are saved as NPZ; every paper table is regenerated from
saved NPZs with no retraining required.
