\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

The methods we report are designed for small-$N$ from-scratch
scientific machine learning.  We document two failure modes outside
that regime and the protocol choices we did not vary.

\paragraph{Pretrained backbones.}
A pretrained backbone is itself an implicit data prior: large-scale
pretraining has collapsed most cross-sample variation in the learned
features.  Twin-indep adds the same prior on top of finetuning, and
the two pull the model toward an over-averaged solution.  Empirically,
on Waterbirds (ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50, $\lambda{=}300$ as
applied to chemistry) accuracy collapses by $27$pp while churn falls.
The failure reproduces inside chemistry: fine-tuning ChemBERTa-77M-MTR
on six headline datasets at the same $\lambda{=}300$ drops accuracy
by $9\text{--}17$pp on every dataset, and \emph{increases} argmax
churn on five of six (Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}).  Bagging
adds no second prior and is unaffected on Waterbirds ($+2$pp accuracy,
$-51\%$ relative churn).  Twin-indep at the from-scratch-tuned
$\lambda$ should not be applied unmodified to pretrained-backbone
settings on either modality we tested (vision, SMILES).

\paragraph{Inter-network consistency collapse on MOF-thermal.}
Strong consistency between two networks has a degenerate solution:
both predict the majority class everywhere, so they agree trivially.
On MOF-thermal, twin-indep and codistillation reach this solution
(id-acc $0.53$, chance on a binary task; ERM $0.62$); bagging and
ERM, neither of which couples the two networks, are unaffected
(Appendix~\ref{app:borderline}).  Twin-indep should not be applied
to small-$N$ datasets whose ERM accuracy is close to the
majority-class baseline; the headline filter ($\text{ERM-acc} \geq
\text{majority-acc} + 5\text{pp}$) is necessary but not sufficient
on its own to predict this collapse.

\paragraph{Architecture-dependent $\lambda$.}
On a non-fingerprint architecture (a $3$-layer GIN on RDKit graphs of
BACE), bagging-$K{=}5$ transfers cleanly: it cuts argmax churn by
$52\%$ ($-11.7$pp [$-12.7$, $-10.7$]) and improves id-accuracy by
$+3.5$pp.  Twin-indep at the BACE-MLP-frozen $\lambda{=}300$ drops
id-accuracy by $15$pp and is rejected by the same accuracy-tolerance
rule that selected $\lambda{=}300$ in the first place; re-running
the rule on BACE-GIN selects $\lambda{=}10$, which cuts argmax
churn $48\%$ at no accuracy cost (Appendix~\ref{app:gin}).  The
selection \emph{rule} is architecture-agnostic; the numerical value
of $\lambda$ is not.  Bagging is unaffected.

\paragraph{Untested settings and protocol choices.}
The headline experiments use binary classification, Morgan
fingerprints, and a $256$-unit MLP from scratch; the test-set
construction is fixed at canonical seed $99$ and $\lambda$ is selected
on BACE alone.  We do not vary the test-set seed, the development
dataset, the twin head count, or the multi-class output type.  An appendix regression study (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity;
Appendix~\ref{app:regression}) shows the methods extend to continuous
targets and rank the same way as on classification: at matched
$2\times$-ERM compute, twin-indep cuts prediction-difference fragility
$39\text{--}42\%$ vs.\ ERM while bagging-$K{=}2$ cuts $29\text{--}32\%$
(twin-indep wins on every dataset).  Bagging-$K{=}5$ at $5\times$-ERM
compute is strongest at $55\text{--}58\%$.  The bagging-$K{=}5$ finding is
paired across $\binom{10}{2}=45$ seed pairs and significant at $p <
10^{-4}$ on the Friedman test (Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}); the
twin-indep finding is more sensitive --- it depends on the
consistency-loss coupling that fails on MOF-thermal and
pretrained-backbone settings, and on a $\lambda$ that does not
transfer across architectures unmodified.
