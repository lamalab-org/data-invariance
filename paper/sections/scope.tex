\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Pretrained backbones (Waterbirds).}
We test the BACE-frozen recipe on Waterbirds, a vision benchmark with
ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50.  Bagging $K{=}5$ improves both
accuracy ($+2$pp) and cross-sample churn ($-51\%$ relative).
Twin-indep at the same $\lambda{=}300$ collapses accuracy by $27$pp
while still reducing churn.
Reducing $\lambda$ to a principled scaled value $\lambda_N
= \lambda_{\mathrm{dev}} \cdot N_{\mathrm{dev}}/N \approx 60$ recovers
some accuracy but does not return to the Pareto frontier of bagging.

\paragraph{Why the consistency loss interferes with finetuning.}
A pretrained backbone is itself an implicit data prior: large-scale
pretraining has already collapsed most cross-sample variation in the
learned features.  Our consistency loss imposes the same prior on top
of finetuning, and the two appear to pull the model toward an
over-averaged solution.  Bagging adds no such second prior and so
extends to the pretrained-backbone setting unchanged.

\paragraph{Consistency-loss accuracy collapse (MOF-thermal).}
We initially included MOF-thermal in the headline benchmarks but
demoted it after observing that twin-indep and codistillation drive
id-test accuracy from ERM's $0.62$ down to $0.53$ on this dataset
(chance level on a binary task), while bagging and ERM are unaffected.
The corresponding ``churn reduction'' is then largely majority-class
prediction, not stabilisation.  We do not claim MOF-thermal as
evidence for either method; it is reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:borderline}.  This is a real failure mode of strong
inter-network consistency on small-$N$ datasets where the two
networks can settle into a uniform prior; the same does not occur on
the seven other chemistry benchmarks we tested.

\paragraph{Architecture and featurisation.}
The chemistry experiments use Morgan fingerprints (radius $2$, $2048$
bits) and a $256$-unit MLP trained from scratch --- the canonical
small-$N$ tabular pipeline.  Whether the twin-indep failure mode on
pretrained backbones (above) also occurs in chemistry under
fine-tuned ChemBERTa~\citep{TODO_chithrananda2020_chemberta} is a
controlled test that we report on a subset of our datasets; the
result is summarised in
Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}.  D-MPNN~\citep{TODO_yang2019_dmpnn}
is the natural follow-up architecture, not run here.

\paragraph{Class of learners.}
Our results are for binary classification with cross-entropy training.
The fragility definition reduces unchanged to multi-class argmax
disagreement and a multi-class symmetric KL, and extends to regression
with a metric substitution (MAE or covered-interval).  We have run
neither, and do not claim the magnitudes reported here transfer.

\paragraph{Twin head count.}
We report twin-indep with $K{=}2$.  Larger $K$ extends mechanically
through all-pairs sym-KL consistency, interpolating between bagging-$K$
and the $K{=}2$ twin we study; we do not characterise the trade-off
empirically.

\paragraph{Single canonical seed.}
The reported numbers fix test-set construction at a single canonical
seed (\texttt{99}); the cross-sample variation is entirely on the
training-bootstrap side.  We do not re-run the sweep at additional
canonical seeds, so we cannot rule out test-set-construction
sensitivity in absolute fragility magnitudes.

\paragraph{Single development dataset.}
The hyperparameter $\lambda$ was selected on BACE alone and frozen.
Cross-validating the development rule across multiple dev datasets
would test robustness of the selection rule itself; we leave this to
follow-up.

\paragraph{Compute used.}
Small-$N$ chemistry experiments (BACE, BBBP, TADF, MOFs, TDC ADME/Tox
benchmarks) run end-to-end on a single laptop CPU; the total compute
budget for the small-$N$ portion of the paper is approximately $50$
CPU-hours.  Waterbirds (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) runs on a single
A100 GPU with a budget of approximately $6$ GPU-hours total.  Each
\texttt{cross\_sample\_train.py} run prints reproducibility metadata
(canonical seed, train seed, mode, $\lambda$).  All trained
predictions are saved as NPZ; every paper table is regenerated from
saved NPZs with no retraining required.
