\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Domain coverage.}  The headline method comparison is on
chemistry binary-classification benchmarks (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME and
Tox, materials-science).  Waterbirds (vision) is the only
non-chemistry test of the rule's transfer; CivilComments / MultiNLI
(text) and the broader WILDS suite are out of scope.  We expect the
mechanism (cross-bootstrap variance is the load-bearing axis at
small $N$) to apply wherever training-pool size is the binding
constraint, but we do not demonstrate it.

\paragraph{Classification-centric main comparison.}  Methods are
compared on cross-sample fragility for binary classification.  Three
regression benchmarks (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity;
Appendix~\ref{app:regression}) confirm the methods extend to
continuous targets and rank the same way, but the headline magnitudes
and the figures are classification-only.  Multi-class classification,
multi-target regression, and structured outputs (segmentation,
generation) are unstudied.

\paragraph{Architectural and protocol scope.}  The main experiments
use a $256$-unit MLP from scratch on Morgan fingerprints; pretrained
backbones (ChemBERTa, ResNet-50) and the from-scratch GIN are
single-canonical-seed cross-checks
(Appendices~\ref{app:gin}--\ref{app:waterbirds_lambda}), not full
sweeps.  We do not vary the twin head count beyond $K{=}2$ or the
development dataset for $\lambda$ selection.

\paragraph{Hyperparameter transfer.}  The numerical value of $\lambda$
does not transfer across architectures unmodified, though the
selection rule does (Section~\ref{sec:generalisation}); a practitioner
moving to a new architecture re-runs a single sweep on a development
dataset.  Bagging has no $\lambda$ and works on every architecture
and backbone we tested.
