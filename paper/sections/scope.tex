\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The main magnitudes and method comparison are on chemistry binary
classification (MoleculeNet, TDC ADME and Tox, materials-science).
Regression is covered for three MoleculeNet benchmarks
(Appendix~\ref{app:regression}); Waterbirds is a single vision
cross-check; multi-class classification and structured outputs are
not tested.  We use $K{=}2$ throughout twin-bootstrap and do not study larger head
counts.  

We measure cross-sample churn at the prediction layer, and at the
top-$\boTopkK$ ranking layer that gates a single
Bayesian-optimisation acquisition step (Appendix~\ref{app:bo_topk}:
under a fixed candidate library, twin-bootstrap raises top-$\boTopkK$
Jaccard between two bootstrap retrainings on every chemistry
dataset).  Whether
this static input-stability translates to better end-to-end
outcomes in a full BO or active-learning loop --- where two
divergent trajectories acquire different molecules in early rounds
and from then on see different candidate pools --- is plausible but
untested.
