\section{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

The methods we report are designed for small-$N$ from-scratch
scientific machine learning.  We document one observation we cannot
explain (MOF-thermal collapse), two settings where the
from-scratch-tuned $\lambda$ does not transfer but the selection
rule recovers the result (architecture, pretrained backbone), and
the protocol choices we did not vary.

\paragraph{Pretrained backbones.}
A pretrained backbone is an implicit data prior; the from-scratch
$\lambda$ is too strong for what the backbone already encodes.
Empirically, on Waterbirds (ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50,
$\lambda{=}300$ as applied to chemistry) accuracy collapses by
$27$pp while churn falls; the failure reproduces on ChemBERTa-77M-MTR
fine-tuned on six headline datasets, where $\lambda{=}300$ drops
accuracy $9\text{--}17$pp and \emph{increases} argmax churn on five
of six.  Re-applying the same $0.02$-tolerance selection rule on
BACE-ChemBERTa picks $\lambda{=}10$, at which twin-indep preserves
accuracy (within $2$pp of ERM) and cuts churn $15\text{--}82\%$ on
every ChemBERTa dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}).  The
rule transfers across architectures and modalities; the numerical
$\lambda$ does not.  We have not re-run Waterbirds under the rule;
bagging is independent of $\lambda$ choice and works on every
backbone we tested ($+2$pp accuracy, $-51\%$ relative churn on
Waterbirds).

\paragraph{MOF-thermal collapse.}
On MOF-thermal, twin-indep and codistillation reach an id-accuracy
of $0.53$ (majority baseline $0.52$); ERM and bagging hold accuracy
($0.62$).  We report the observation but cannot say why MOF-thermal
differs from the other eight datasets in the magnitudes table from
the available evidence (Appendix~\ref{app:borderline}).

\paragraph{Architecture-dependent $\lambda$.}
On a non-fingerprint architecture (a $3$-layer GIN on RDKit graphs of
BACE), bagging-$K{=}5$ transfers cleanly: it cuts argmax churn by
$52\%$ ($-11.7$pp [$-12.7$, $-10.7$]) and improves id-accuracy by
$+3.5$pp.  Twin-indep at the BACE-MLP-frozen $\lambda{=}300$ drops
id-accuracy by $15$pp and is rejected by the same accuracy-tolerance
rule that selected $\lambda{=}300$ in the first place; re-running
the rule on BACE-GIN selects $\lambda{=}10$, which cuts argmax
churn $48\%$ at no accuracy cost (Appendix~\ref{app:gin}).  The
selection \emph{rule} is architecture-agnostic; the numerical value
of $\lambda$ is not.  Bagging is unaffected.

\paragraph{Untested settings and protocol choices.}
The headline experiments use binary classification, Morgan
fingerprints, and a $256$-unit MLP from scratch; the test-set
construction is fixed at canonical seed $99$ and $\lambda$ is selected
on BACE alone.  We do not vary the test-set seed, the development
dataset, the twin head count, or the multi-class output type.  An appendix regression study (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity;
Appendix~\ref{app:regression}) shows the methods extend to continuous
targets and rank the same way as on classification: at matched
$2\times$-ERM compute, twin-indep cuts prediction-difference fragility
$39\text{--}42\%$ vs.\ ERM while bagging-$K{=}2$ cuts $29\text{--}32\%$
(twin-indep wins on every dataset).  Bagging-$K{=}5$ at $5\times$-ERM
compute is strongest at $55\text{--}58\%$.  The bagging-$K{=}5$ finding is
paired across $\binom{10}{2}=45$ seed pairs and significant at $p <
10^{-4}$ on the Friedman test (Appendix~\ref{app:friedman}); the
twin-indep finding is more sensitive --- it depends on a $\lambda$
that does not transfer across architectures unmodified (though the
selection rule does), and the MOF-thermal observation remains
unexplained.
