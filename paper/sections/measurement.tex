\section{The phenomenon}
\label{sec:measurement}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
We measure cross-sample fragility across twelve standard
small-$N$ chemistry benchmarks spanning ${\sim}500$ to ${\sim}20\,000$
training examples.

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{MoleculeNet} (via TDC): BACE, BBBP.
  \item \textbf{TDC ADME}: HIA\_Hou, Bioavailability\_Ma,
    Pgp\_Broccatelli, BBB\_Martins.
  \item \textbf{TDC Tox}: hERG, DILI, AMES, Skin\_Reaction.
  \item \textbf{Materials science}: TADF (organic light-emission),
    MOF-thermal-stability, MOF-solvent-removal, perovskite formation
    energy, battery capacity.
\end{itemize}
Featurisation is Morgan fingerprints (radius 2, 2048 bits) for
molecular datasets and dataset-specific descriptors for materials.
Architecture is a 256-unit MLP trained from scratch in all cases.

\subsection{Fragility magnitudes are large and dataset-specific}
\label{sec:fragility-magnitudes}

Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes} reports mean cross-bootstrap
fragility (sym-KL between two ERM bootstraps on the canonical id-test)
and argmax churn for each dataset, alongside training-set size $N$ and
ERM accuracy.  Argmax churn ranges from $8.0\%$ (BBB-Martins) to
$16.8\%$ (DILI); sym-KL varies by ${\sim}4\times$ across datasets at
similar $N$ (TADF $0.27$ vs.\ BACE $0.75$ at $N \approx 1000$),
reflecting intrinsic differences in feature dimension, label noise,
and task difficulty rather than a single $N$-dependent rate.  We
therefore do not fit a cross-dataset $1/N$ curve; algorithmic stability
is the \emph{motivation} for fragility decreasing with $N$, not an
empirical claim we verify across heterogeneous tasks.

Within a single task (BACE), subsampling the training pool to $M \in
\{200, 400, 600, 800, 968\}$ traces a within-dataset $N$-scaling curve
(Appendix~\ref{app:nscaling}, Table~\ref{tab:nscaling}): fragility
decreases over $M=200 \to 800$ with a log-log slope of $-0.22$, then
levels off at an irreducible floor.  This is shallower than the
theoretical $\beta = O(1/N)$ rate ($-1$); Appendix~\ref{app:theory}
enumerates the gaps that prevent the bound from transferring
quantitatively.

\subsection{Fragility predicts which predictions will flip}
\label{sec:churn-decile}

Per-example fragility identifies the predictions that retraining will
flip, and does so better than a single-model softmax-confidence
baseline (Appendix~\ref{app:entropy}).  Figure~\ref{fig:decile} ranks
every test example by its mean
cross-bootstrap fragility across all ERM seed pairs, then bins into
deciles and reports the average argmax-flip rate within each decile.
On every dataset we tested, the bottom $50\%$-fragility examples flip
at $0\%$; deciles $6$--$8$ rise sharply; the top decile flips at
$41\text{--}47\%$.  A deployed system can therefore flag the most
fragile predictions for re-review without retraining a cascade of
models---one additional bootstrap suffices to compute per-example
fragility.
