\section{Cross-sample prediction fragility}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
\label{sec:measurement}

\paragraph{Setting.}
A learner $\mathcal{A}$ maps a training set
$S = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ drawn iid from population $\mathcal{D}$
to a classifier $f_S = \mathcal{A}(S)$ that returns a class
$\hat{y}(x) = \arg\max_c f_S(x)_c$ at each test example $x$.
Cross-sample fragility quantifies how $\hat{y}(x)$ changes when $S$
is replaced by an independent iid draw from $\mathcal{D}$.

\paragraph{Definition.}
Given two iid training samples $S_A, S_B \sim \mathcal{D}^N$ and a
test example $x$, the \emph{cross-sample fragility} of $\mathcal{A}$ at
$x$ is
\begin{equation}
  \rho(\mathcal{A}, x) =
  \mathbb{P}_{S_A, S_B}\!\big[\arg\max f_{S_A}(x) \neq \arg\max f_{S_B}(x)\big].
\end{equation}
A fragility-aware report supplements the standard accuracy with the
expected per-example argmax disagreement
$\bar\rho(\mathcal{A}) = \mathbb{E}_x [\rho(\mathcal{A}, x)]$, and
optionally with a distributional analogue
$\bar\rho_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{A})
= \mathbb{E}_x \big[\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{KL}(f_{S_A}(x) \| f_{S_B}(x))
                                + \mathrm{KL}(f_{S_B}(x) \| f_{S_A}(x)))\big]$.
Throughout the paper, ``fragility'' is the conceptual name for
$\bar\rho$; ``class-flip rate'' is its operational expression as a
percentage and is what figure axes and table cells display.  When we
need to connect to prior literature on prediction churn we use
``argmax churn'' (\S\ref{sec:related}); the three names refer to the
same quantity.

\paragraph{Imbalance, accuracy, and other aggregate metrics.}
Accuracy on imbalanced data can mask class-specific drift: two
models that swap predictions on the minority class while agreeing on
the majority can post near-identical accuracy and very different
precision or recall.  Every aggregate metric (accuracy, AUROC,
precision, recall, $F_1$, AP) is a population average that
summarises out per-example variation; cross-sample fragility is the
per-example complement, counting disagreements before any averaging.

We pre-screen every dataset to ERM accuracy $\geq$ majority-class
baseline $+\preregFilterPP$pp (\S\ref{sec:datasets}), so the gap we measure is
learned-decision performance rather than majority-class
shuffling.  Appendix~\ref{app:additional_metrics} reports paired
$|\Delta\text{precision}|$, $|\Delta\text{recall}|$, $|\Delta F_1|$,
and $|\Delta\text{AP}|$ between two retrainings alongside
$|\Delta\text{acc}|$.  On the imbalanced ADME tasks, recall and
precision drifts reach $2\text{--}3\times$ $|\Delta\text{acc}|$
(cyp2d6\_substrate: $|\Delta\text{recall}|=\cypTwoDeltaRecall$\,pp
vs.\ $|\Delta\text{acc}|=\cypTwoDeltaAcc$\,pp), so accuracy alone does undersell the
inter-retraining variation.  Even so, the per-example argmax-churn
rate exceeds every aggregate-metric drift on every dataset,
typically by $2\text{--}10\times$.  Per-example disagreement is the
quantity that drives next-step decisions in scientific ML, and it
dominates aggregate-metric drift under any of these summary
statistics.  Minority-class predictions are
$2\text{--}4\times$ more unstable across retrainings than
majority-class predictions on the most imbalanced datasets
(BBB-Martins, BBBP, CYP2D6-Sub; Appendix~\ref{app:per_class_churn}):
the disagreement concentrates on the class practitioners care about.

\paragraph{Churn correlates with task difficulty, but is not
explained by it.}
At the dataset level, the mean class-flip rate correlates with mean ERM
accuracy at Pearson $\churnAccCorrPearson$ (Spearman
$\churnAccCorrSpearman$) across the \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry
benchmarks: harder datasets show more churn.  This is expected --- a
less-certain decision boundary is crossed in different places by two
retrainings.  It is not a confound.  The data-side methods we
evaluate reduce churn on every dataset regardless of difficulty
(Figure~\ref{fig:main}).  The pattern is not a side effect of
accuracy improvement: deep ensembles raise mean accuracy by
$\deepEnsAccDelta$\,pp without reducing churn (Table~\ref{tab:main}),
while twin-bootstrap cuts churn by a median
$\twinMedianReduction\%$ at a slight accuracy degradation
($\twinAccDelta$\,pp on average) --- the strongest churn reducer is
also among the weaker accuracy improvers, and the strongest
accuracy improvers do not consistently reduce churn.  At the
per-example level, fragility captures retraining-flip risk better
than predictive entropy (Appendix~\ref{app:entropy}).  Churn is not
a recoding of accuracy.

\paragraph{Bootstrap estimator.}
A practitioner has one observed training set $S$, not a population
$\mathcal{D}$.  We estimate $\rho$ by drawing two independent
bootstraps from $S$ (size $N$, with replacement); each bootstrap is
a draw from the empirical distribution $\hat{\mathcal{D}}_S$, which
converges to $\mathcal{D}$ as $N \to \infty$.  All
numerical magnitudes we report are with respect to this bootstrap
proxy --- the quantity computable from a single observed training
set.

\paragraph{Measurement protocol.}
A single fixed seed --- which we call the \emph{canonical} seed
--- determines the train/test split once and for all; the resulting
training pool and test set are reused across every method and every
train seed, so test-side variance is zero by construction within a
canonical seed.  For each train seed $s \in \{1, \dots, \nSeeds\}$ we
draw a bootstrap $S^{(s)}$ from the canonical training pool and train
$f^{(s)} = \mathcal{A}(S^{(s)})$ on it.  Cross-sample churn is the average of
$\mathbf{1}[\arg\max f^{(s)}(x) \neq \arg\max f^{(s')}(x)]$ over all
$\binom{\nSeeds}{2}{=}\nSeedPairs$ pairs $(s, s')$ and over the canonical test set.
Confidence intervals come from $10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the
seed-pair distribution; cross-method deltas are paired.

We replicate the entire protocol on
$\nCanonicalSeeds$ independent canonical seeds
($\canonicalSeed$, $7$, and $42$).  Magnitudes quoted in prose are
across-seed means; the development $\lambda$-selection rule selects
$\lambda{=}300$ on every canonical seed independently
(Appendix~\ref{app:seed_sensitivity}).  Tables in the main paper
display the $\canonicalSeed$ canonical-seed view with within-seed
paired-bootstrap CIs; Appendix table~\ref{tab:seed_sensitivity}
reports the per-seed value of every cell in Table~\ref{tab:main}.
Single-seed estimates of cross-sample churn vary by up to
$\maxAcrossSeedChurnSpreadPP$\,pp on individual (dataset, method)
cells, comparable to the across-dataset spread, while paired
$\Delta$-churn vs.\ ERM is much more stable across canonical seeds
(largest across-seed range $\maxAcrossSeedDeltaSpreadPP$\,pp on a
single cell, typically a small fraction of the magnitude of the
reduction).  Pretrained-backbone appendices (GIN, ChemBERTa,
Waterbirds) are reported on canonical seed $\canonicalSeed$ only.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
\label{sec:datasets}
We measure cross-sample fragility on seventeen chemistry benchmarks
($N \in [\,304, 4658\,]$) drawn from
MoleculeNet~\citep{Wu2018} (BACE, BBBP, ClinTox),
the Therapeutics Data Commons (TDC)~\citep{huang2021therapeutics} ADME
suite (HIA\_Hou, Bioavailability\_Ma, Pgp\_Broccatelli, BBB\_Martins,
CYP2C9-Sub, CYP2D6-Sub, CYP3A4-Sub), TDC Tox (hERG, DILI, AMES,
Skin\_Reaction), and materials-science benchmarks
(TADF~\citep{jablonka2026clever, Huang2024},
MOF-thermal-stability,
MOF-solvent-removal~\citep{jablonka2026clever,Nandy2022}).  A pre-registered health
filter (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) keeps the nine datasets on which
ERM exceeds the majority-class baseline by at least $\preregFilterPP$pp
and the canonical id-test set has at least $\preregMinTestSize$ examples
(Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes}, top group).  Three datasets
on which ERM exceeds majority by only $\borderlineGapMin$--$\borderlineGapMax$pp
on a $\borderlineNTestMin$--$\borderlineNTestMax$-example test set
(Skin\_Reaction, hERG, HIA\_Hou) are reported in the same table under
a separate group; we do not run the method comparison on them because
the small test sets do not give enough statistical power.  Five
datasets are excluded because ERM does not exceed majority at the
$\preregFilterPP$pp threshold (Bioavailability\_Ma, MOF\_solvent,
CYP2C9-Sub, CYP3A4-Sub, ClinTox); their per-dataset filter outcomes
are tabulated in Appendix~\ref{app:repro} (Table~\ref{tab:filter-outcomes}).
Featurisation is dataset-specific.  The MoleculeNet binary tasks
(BACE, BBBP, ClinTox) and the TDC ADME/Tox tasks (DILI,
Pgp\_Broccatelli, BBB\_Martins, AMES, CYP2C9-Sub, CYP2D6-Sub,
CYP3A4-Sub, hERG, HIA\_Hou, Skin\_Reaction, Bioavailability\_Ma)
use $2048$-bit Morgan fingerprints at radius $2$, computed via
RDKit~\citep{rdkit}.  TADF uses the same Morgan fingerprints
concatenated with $217$ RDKit-derived molecular descriptors
(molecular weight, EState indices, partial charges, etc.).  MOF-thermal-stability and
MOF-solvent-removal use $174$ Revised Auto-Correlation (RAC)
descriptors over metal-centre, linker, functional-group, and
ligand-chemistry atoms at depths $0$--$3$, plus geometric surface
area (GSA), the standard featurisation for MOF property
prediction~\citep{Moosavi2020}.  All inputs are
column-standardised before training.  The default architecture used
throughout the main comparison is a $256$-unit MLP trained from
scratch on these features.  Appendices~\ref{app:gin} and~\ref{app:chemberta_scope}
report cross-checks on a graph network and on ChemBERTa-77M-MTR.
