\section{Cross-sample prediction fragility}
\label{sec:preliminaries}
\label{sec:measurement}

\paragraph{Setting.}
A learner $\mathcal{A}$ maps a training set
$S = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ drawn iid from population $\mathcal{D}$
to a classifier $f_S = \mathcal{A}(S)$ that returns a class
$\hat{y}(x) = \arg\max_c f_S(x)_c$ at each test example $x$.
Cross-sample fragility quantifies how $\hat{y}(x)$ changes when $S$
is replaced by an independent iid draw from $\mathcal{D}$.

\paragraph{Definition.}
Given two iid training samples $S_A, S_B \sim \mathcal{D}^N$ and a
test example $x$, the \emph{cross-sample fragility} of $\mathcal{A}$ at
$x$ is
\begin{equation}
  \rho(\mathcal{A}, x) =
  \mathbb{P}_{S_A, S_B}\!\big[\arg\max f_{S_A}(x) \neq \arg\max f_{S_B}(x)\big].
\end{equation}
A fragility-aware report supplements the standard accuracy with the
expected per-example argmax disagreement
$\bar\rho(\mathcal{A}) = \mathbb{E}_x [\rho(\mathcal{A}, x)]$, and
optionally with a distributional analogue
$\bar\rho_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathcal{A})
= \mathbb{E}_x \big[\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathrm{KL}(f_{S_A}(x) \| f_{S_B}(x))
                                + \mathrm{KL}(f_{S_B}(x) \| f_{S_A}(x)))\big]$.
Throughout the paper, ``fragility'' refers to $\bar\rho$ unless
otherwise stated, and ``argmax churn'' is the same quantity reported
as a percentage; figures use ``id-churn'' on axes when the canonical
id-test set is the population the expectation is taken over.

\paragraph{Bootstrap estimator.}
A practitioner has one observed training set $S$, not a population
$\mathcal{D}$.  We estimate $\rho$ by drawing two independent
bootstraps from $S$ (size $N$, with replacement); each bootstrap is
a draw from the empirical distribution
$\hat{\mathcal{D}}_S \to \mathcal{D}$ as $N \to \infty$.  All
numerical magnitudes we report are with respect to this bootstrap
proxy --- the quantity computable from a single observed training
set.

\paragraph{Connection to algorithmic stability.}
\citet{TODO_bousquet2002} define uniform stability $\beta$ on the
loss when a single training example is altered.  Cross-sample
fragility is the corresponding output-space quantity under
multi-example perturbation, and decreases with $N$ for the same
reason $\beta$ does.  We use $\beta$ as the interpretive anchor but
do not claim fragility matches the $O(1/N)$ rate quantitatively;
Appendix~\ref{app:theory} enumerates the gaps.

\paragraph{Measurement protocol.}
A single canonical seed fixes the train/test split.
For each train-side seed $s \in \{1, \dots, 10\}$ we draw a bootstrap
$S^{(s)}$ from the canonical training set and train
$f^{(s)} = \mathcal{A}(S^{(s)})$ on it; the test set is byte-identical
across $s$.  Cross-sample churn is the average of
$\mathbf{1}[\arg\max f^{(s)}(x) \neq \arg\max f^{(s')}(x)]$ over all
$\binom{10}{2}=45$ pairs $(s, s')$ and over the canonical test set.
Confidence intervals come from $10{,}000$-sample bootstrap on the
seed-pair distribution; cross-method deltas are paired.

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}
We measure cross-sample fragility on seventeen chemistry benchmarks
($N \in [\,304, 4658\,]$) drawn from MoleculeNet (BACE, BBBP,
ClinTox), TDC ADME (HIA\_Hou, Bioavailability\_Ma, Pgp\_Broccatelli,
BBB\_Martins, CYP2C9-Sub, CYP2D6-Sub, CYP3A4-Sub), TDC Tox (hERG,
DILI, AMES, Skin\_Reaction), and materials science (TADF,
MOF-thermal-stability, MOF-solvent-removal).  A pre-registered health
filter (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) keeps the nine datasets on which
ERM exceeds the majority-class baseline by at least $5$pp and the
canonical id-test set has at least $60$ examples (Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes}).
Three small-test-set datasets are reported only in an appendix table
(Skin\_Reaction, hERG, HIA\_Hou); five are excluded entirely because
ERM does not exceed majority at the $5$pp threshold (Bioavailability\_Ma,
MOF\_solvent, CYP2C9-Sub, CYP3A4-Sub, ClinTox).
Featurisation is dataset-specific.  The MoleculeNet binary tasks
(BACE, BBBP, ClinTox) and the TDC ADME/Tox tasks (DILI,
Pgp\_Broccatelli, BBB\_Martins, AMES, CYP2C9-Sub, CYP2D6-Sub,
CYP3A4-Sub, hERG, HIA\_Hou, Skin\_Reaction, Bioavailability\_Ma)
use $2048$-bit Morgan fingerprints at radius $2$, computed via
RDKit~\citep{TODO_rdkit}.  TADF uses the same Morgan fingerprints
concatenated with $217$ RDKit-derived molecular descriptors
(molecular weight, EState indices, partial charges, etc.) from the
clever-materials-hans pipeline.  MOF-thermal-stability and
MOF-solvent-removal use $174$ Revised Auto-Correlation (RAC)
descriptors over metal-centre, linker, functional-group, and
ligand-chemistry atoms at depths $0$--$3$, plus geometric surface
area (GSA), the standard featurisation for MOF property
prediction~\citep{TODO_moosavi2020_rac}.  All inputs are
column-standardised before training.  The headline architecture is
a $256$-unit MLP trained from scratch on these features.
Appendices~\ref{app:gin} and~\ref{app:chemberta_scope} report
cross-checks on a graph network and on ChemBERTa-77M-MTR.

\subsection{Magnitudes}
\label{sec:fragility-magnitudes}

Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes} reports the cross-bootstrap
fragility and argmax churn for each of the nine health-passing
datasets alongside training-set size $N$ and ERM accuracy.  Argmax
churn ranges from $8.0\%$ (BBB-Martins) to $21.8\%$ (MOF-thermal).  Sym-KL
varies by ${\sim}4\times$ across datasets at similar $N$ (TADF $0.27$
vs.\ BACE $0.75$ at $N \approx 1000$), so we do not fit a
cross-dataset $1/N$ curve; algorithmic stability motivates the
decrease of fragility with $N$ within a single task, not an
asymptotic relation across heterogeneous ones.
