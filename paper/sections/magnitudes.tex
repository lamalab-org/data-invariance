\section{Cross-sample fragility flips $\churnMin$--$\churnMax\%$ of test predictions on \nDatasetsHeadline{} chemistry benchmarks}
\label{sec:fragility-magnitudes}

Table~\ref{tab:fragility-magnitudes} reports the cross-bootstrap
class-flip rate (argmax churn) and the corresponding distributional
gap (sym-KL) for each of the \nDatasetsHeadline{} health-passing
datasets, alongside training-set size $N$, ERM accuracy, and the mean
pairwise accuracy difference $|\Delta\text{acc}|$ between two
retrainings.  Class-flip rate ranges from $\churnMin\%$ (BBB-Martins)
to $\churnMax\%$ (MOF-thermal).  Aggregate accuracy moves
$\accDiffMin\text{--}\accDiffMax$\,pp between two retrainings while
individual predictions disagree at
$\churnAccRatioMin\text{--}\churnAccRatioMax\times$ that rate.
Sym-KL varies by ${\sim}\symKLDatasetSpread\times$ across datasets,
and the variation is task-dependent rather than a function of
training-set size alone (e.g.\ TADF and BACE both have
$N \approx 1000$ but differ by ${\sim}2\times$ in sym-KL).

The within-task $N$-scaling on BACE (Appendix~\ref{app:nscaling})
is monotone decreasing for $M \in \{200, 400, 600, 800\}$ with a
log-log slope of $-0.22$ and an irreducible floor --- fragility
decreases with training-pool size but not to zero.  Three regression
benchmarks (ESOL, FreeSolv, Lipophilicity; Appendix~\ref{app:regression})
show the analogue: the per-example absolute prediction difference
between two retrainings is $\regFragRatioLow\text{--}\regFragRatioHigh\%$
of the deployed model's mean absolute error, so a single retraining
moves predictions by roughly half the test-set MAE on average.  Both the classification
class-flip rate and the regression prediction-difference fragility
are what data-side methods should reduce.
